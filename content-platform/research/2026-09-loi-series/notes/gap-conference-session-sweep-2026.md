### 2.3 Post-event analysis on KM and data
**Harbor brief, "ILTACON 2026: Why AI usage isn't the same as AI value"**, Justin Farmer (Practice Group Leader, Enterprise Solutions), 2026-09-11. Consultancy (VENDOR-class), verified verbatim. https://harborglobal.com/insights/briefs/iltacon-2026-why-ai-usage-isnt-the-same-as-ai-value/
- "A recurring theme across ILTACON's marketing technology and knowledge management sessions was that clean, connected data is the precondition for AI producing anything useful."
- "point it at a broken one and it will still return a confident answer, just a wrong one."
- The sequence: "connect the systems so tools can talk to each other, govern the data and establish trust in it, then apply AI for precision."
- Practical tactic: "govern only the data behind a specific, recurring deliverable, such as a report produced regularly or a request fielded constantly, and expand from there."
- **KM's role in AI governance (directly relevant to Article 5):** "A recurring frustration raised in roundtable discussions was fragmented ownership of AI security and governance decisions across IT, Risk, the General Counsel's office, and knowledge management". Harbor's conclusion: "Governance needs a clear owner and a defined, visible conversation."
- Measurement: "Stop reporting logins and usage rates." Harbor's own report found "0% of participating top-tier law firms and global in-house departments have a mature framework for measuring AI's business impact, despite a 41% increase in average annual law firm software spending between 2021 and 2025." (Harbor's own research; Above the Law's Stephen Embry has questioned its methodological transparency - see earlier notes.)
- Anecdote: "The CIO of a 100-lawyer insurance defense firm described his managing partner ordering Copilot and other AI tools shut off after billing came in down".
- Staffing signal: "Harbor's 266 AI-focused professionals" (self-reported).

## 5. Cross-conference read (my synthesis; label as inference in any article)
1. **Same sentence, three rooms.** CLOC (Snell): "what it has done, what broke, and how we govern it". ILTACON (Harbor; Rotenberg): usage is not value; "trusted progress". RLLB (Calve): decisions "written once and executed again". All three 2026 events moved from adoption to operating model, measurement and governance.
2. **Data is framed as the precondition, not a track.** At ILTACON, KM and marketing-technology sessions converged on "clean, connected data"; vendors answered with context graphs, semantic layers and MCP servers. At CGI, the strongest BI content was Westpac's executive dashboard and invoice-data-to-strategy sessions.
3. **KM is being re-described as taxonomy, context and governance ownership**, and the evidence is mostly law-firm side (ILTACON, ILTA survey, CCBJ interviews). In-house evidence is thin: one CGI breakout (JPMorganChase, which employs a KM librarian inside legal) and an Academy workshop whose description does not mention AI. iManage's Walker explicitly flags corporate legal departments as having weaker matter records.
4. **Who owns AI governance is unresolved.** Harbor reports ownership "fragmented" across IT, Risk, the GC's office and KM. ILTA's survey says firms expect KM headcount to grow with AI but publishes no number. No 2026 source assigns KM (or legal ops) formal leadership of AI programs.
5. **Intake/self-service is a vendor-led conversation** at CGI (Checkbox "legal front door", Legora Portal, OpenText) with a few practitioner cameos (HPE low-code intake build; iFood at RLLB). The most quotable practitioner detail is iFood's no-human-in-the-loop path for low-risk appendices.

## 6. Distinct viewpoints captured
- Association view (CLOC/Snell): AI conversation has shifted to what broke and how to govern; legal ops "building, proving, and leading".
- Consultancy view (Harbor/Clem, Farmer): near-universal adoption, lagging operating model; measure outcomes not logins; govern data deliverable-by-deliverable; governance needs one owner.
- DMS-vendor view (NetDocuments/Baxter, iManage/Walker): models are rented, institutional context is permanent; "right back at taxonomy"; bring the application to the knowledge.
- Data-platform vendor view (Entegrata/Baldwin): the dedicated data layer debate is over; firms now race to ship one.
- Law-firm practitioner view (Fox, Ogletree): move from efficiency metrics to enablement; time-savings totals are noise unless tied to data.
- Trade-press sceptic/analyst view (Calve; Ambrogi): "good enough" economics and cost per answer; "peak legal tech" and capital misallocation; vendor benchmarks should be read as vendor benchmarks.
- In-house practitioner view (iFood, Salesforce via CCBJ; Westpac, JPMorganChase via CGI agenda): embed thresholds and rules into intake flows; use invoice data to allocate work; executive dashboards that answer what legal costs and what it delivers.
- Survey-author view (ILTA): "The platform migration is largely complete, but the governance migration has yet to begin."
