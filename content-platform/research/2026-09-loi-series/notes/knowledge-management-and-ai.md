### 1.5 CLOC (Core 12) and ACC (Maturity Model) - the in-house frameworks pre-date AI and do not mention it
- CLOC Core 12, Knowledge Management: "Tap the knowledge and capability of your entire organization" / "Implement processes and standards to collect, structure, and organize knowledge to save time and improve outcomes. Leverage existing knowledge to make work easier." No mention of AI anywhere on the Core 12 page. PRIMARY. https://cloc.org/cloc-core-12/ (undated; accessed 2026-09-21)
- ACC Legal Operations Maturity Model 2.0 (PDF read directly), KM definition: "The process of capturing, distributing, and effectively using both structured and tacit knowledge assets, from work products (such as legal memos) to understanding of an issue due to prior experience." Early stage includes: "Seek law firm advice on answers to questions; may be getting advice on same topic repeatedly; no effort to consolidate legal work product so it can be repurposed". Intermediate: "Collaboration between document/process owners and curators to create and manage templates and toolkits"; "At least one staff member partially responsible for KM initiatives". Advanced: "Mechanisms and tools for curation"; "KM a standard component in organizational processes, supported by automated workflows"; "At least one full-time dedicated KM professional"; "Significant KM dedicated budget relative to the organization". No mention of AI. The "advanced" bar for in-house KM headcount is a single full-time person - a useful contrast with law-firm KM departments. PRIMARY. https://www.acc.com/sites/default/files/program-materials/upload/Legal-Opertaitons-Maturity-Model-2.0---ACC.pdf (PDF undated; version 2.0 updates the 2017 original; release year not confirmed in this session)
- Observation for the article (my inference, not a sourced claim): both in-house frameworks still define KM as reuse of work product by humans. Neither has been updated to describe knowledge as AI grounding/evaluation material. That gap is itself an argument the article can make.

## Theme 3. Technical reasons KM matters to AI

- RAG reduces but does not eliminate hallucination, and quality of the retrieved corpus is the controlling variable. Stanford RegLab/HAI, "Hallucination-Free? Assessing the Reliability of Leading AI Legal Research Tools" (Magesh, Surani, Dahl, Suzgun, Manning, Ho; arXiv May 2024; AI Law Librarians refers to it as a 2025 study, which suggests a later journal publication that I did not verify): tools from LexisNexis and Thomson Reuters "each hallucinate between 17% and 33% of the time"; providers had touted RAG as "'eliminating' or 'avoid[ing]' hallucinations". PRIMARY. https://reglab.stanford.edu/publications/hallucination-free-assessing-the-reliability-of-leading-ai-legal-research-tools/ (2024-05). Note: outside the 2025-26 window; use as background only.
- AI Law Librarians (Rebecca Fordon, 2026-02-19) literature review: general-purpose models hallucinated 58-88% on legal questions in the 2024 "Large Legal Fictions" study; specialized RAG tools 17% (Lexis+ AI) to 33% (Westlaw AI-Assisted Research) vs GPT-4 at 43%; legal platforms do better because they search "curated, verified legal databases with citators"; still "roughly one in five responses contains errors, even from top-tier specialized legal tools." TRADE/academic blog. https://www.ailawlibrarians.com/2026/02/19/what-the-science-says-about-hallucinations-in-legal-research/
- Evaluation sets are built from law-firm knowledge. Vals Legal AI Report (VLAIR, 2025-02-27): "The Consortium Firms together created the dataset used for the study" - Reed Smith, Fisher Phillips, McDermott Will & Emery, Ogletree Deakins and four anonymous firms supplied 500+ samples: questions, reference documents, ideal responses and correctness criteria. This is direct evidence of finished work product being repurposed as gold-standard evaluation material. PRIMARY. https://www.vals.ai/industry-reports/vlair-2-27-25
- Playbooks as machine-readable instructions. Anthropic's Legal plugin (launched 2026-02-03 per LawSites/Artificial Lawyer): "automates contract review, NDA triage, compliance workflows, legal briefings, and templated responses - all configurable to your organization's playbook"; playbook is configured "in a local settings file to define standard positions, acceptable ranges, and escalation triggers." PRIMARY. https://claude.com/plugins/legal (undated product page; accessed 2026-09-21)
- Agent skills as the new knowledge primitive (non-legal, but the clearest statement of the idea). Gal Bakal, "Knowledge Activation: AI Skills as the Institutional Knowledge Primitive for Agentic Software Development" (arXiv 2603.14805, submitted 2026-03-16, revised 2026-06-04): "knowledge remains trapped in formats designed for human interpretation"; proposes "Atomic Knowledge Units" that encode "what to do, which tools to use, what constraints to respect". Yahoo deployment, 67 engineers, 2.6 hours/week saved. PRIMARY (preprint; software engineering, not legal). https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14805
- "Context engineering" replacing "prompt engineering" (Ed Walters, Clio) and "context wins" (Winston Weinberg, Harvey) - Artificial Lawyer 2026-01-08 (see 1.4).
- Garbage-in/garbage-out, practitioner voice: Rob Taylor (Tiger Eye) "AI will magnify any data issues you may have, and do so at pace, and at scale." (2025-12-19, see 1.8). Benamram: "AI amplifies the cost of fragmentation." (2026-03, see 1.4).
- Fine-tuning: no 2025-26 source found describing a legal department or firm fine-tuning on its own KM corpus as a mainstream practice; Lupl's "AI law firms to watch" notes most AI-native firms use off-the-shelf LLMs with custom application layers. VENDOR. https://www.lupl.com/blog/10-ai-law-firms-to-watch-in-2026/ (2026, exact date not shown)

## Theme 4. New sources of knowledge

- Womble Bond Dickinson's Tom Humberstone: repositories now go "beyond traditional, curated know-how to encompass a broader set of sources now. This could be transactional content or third-party content as well" (Tiger Eye, 2025-12-19; see 1.8).
- Jus Mundi CEO (Legal IT Insider, 2026-01-15): firms must capture processes, prompts and best practices as knowledge, not only precedents (paraphrase by the fetch tool; see 1.8).
- Benamram's list of what KM must now contain: "precedents, outcomes, expertise signals" (Artificial Lawyer, 2026-01-08).
- ILTACON 2025 roundtable: knowledge "scattered across OneDrive, SharePoint, Teams, and personal folders" (Artificial Lawyer, 2025-08-13) - i.e., chat and collaboration content is part of the de facto corpus.
- Meeting transcripts as a new, risky knowledge source: Mayer Brown, "AI Notetakers: Productivity Tool or Emerging Legal Risk?" (2026-06-03): transcripts create records that "over time... may be accessed by individuals who were not present" and "used for purposes beyond those underpinning the contemporaneous documentation"; broad retention expands legal-hold scope and e-discovery cost. PRIMARY (law-firm client alert). https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/ai-notetakers-productivity-tool-or-emerging-legal-risk
- Redline/negotiation history: only vendor marketing located (e.g., GC AI, Gavel, LegalOn "playbook agents" that convert prior redlines into playbooks). GC AI blog (Josh Bertini, 2026-09-11) cites customer Marvell: "we've auto-triaged, I would say, about 40%" of intake (Cindy Prabhakar). VENDOR. https://gc.ai/blog/ai-in-legal-operations
- Not found with a citable neutral source: billing narratives, decision logs/approvals, outside-counsel work product, and expert corrections of AI output being treated as KM assets. These remain the article's own argument rather than a documented industry practice.

## Theme 6. In-house specifics; legal operations as curator

- In-house adoption has overtaken law firms (47% vs 41%, Thomson Reuters 2026) while the ACC "advanced" standard for KM staffing is one full-time KM professional. In-house teams therefore reach the KM bottleneck with far less KM capacity than firms, which have KM departments, PSLs and CKOs.
- ACC early-stage description is a precise statement of the in-house knowledge-loss problem: "may be getting advice on same topic repeatedly; no effort to consolidate legal work product so it can be repurposed".
- Legal-ops remit in AI is framed as governance: Legal.io (2026-03-06): "For legal operations professionals, the near-term priority is governance rather than product evaluation."; also names a "Verification Tax" on in-house teams auditing AI output. ## Theme 7. Risks

- Privilege: United States v. Heppner, No. 25 Cr. 503 (JSR) (S.D.N.Y.; bench ruling 2026-02-10, opinion 2026-02-17), Judge Rakoff: exchanges with a consumer gen-AI tool not protected by privilege or work product; "Because Claude is not an attorney... that alone disposes of Heppner's claim of privilege." Debevoise (2026-02-11) takeaways: use enterprise tools with confidentiality terms, document that work is at counsel's direction, improve privilege logs. PRIMARY-adjacent (law-firm analysis of a court ruling). https://www.debevoisedatablog.com/2026/02/11/district-court-rules-ai-generated-documents-are-not-protected-by-privilege/ ; see also https://harvardlawreview.org/blog/2026/03/united-states-v-heppner/ (2026-03)
- Ethical walls and RAG: Hintyr (Alexander Cohan, 2026-03-27): "Vector embeddings strip away the permission metadata that traditional document management systems rely on." Cites CrowdStrike research and a January 2026 Microsoft advisory (CW1226324) where Copilot "bypassed sensitivity labels for nearly a month". VENDOR blog; underlying Microsoft advisory not independently verified. https://www.hintyr.com/blog/ethical-walls-ai-law-firms
- Access vs security tension: Tiger Eye (2025-12-19): "Security was once a secondary concern in knowledge systems, but it is now deeply embedded." (Humberstone); need-to-know lockdown means "you have to work even harder at your knowledge management" (Tellyn).
- Stale content: LexisNexis interviewee (2026-01-06): "Even if we find the right precedent, sometimes it's not easy to update it according to new case laws."
- Provenance: ClearPeople (2025-12-02): "Provenance and auditability become operational requirements".
- Retention / discoverability of new knowledge sources: Mayer Brown (2026-06-03) on AI notetaker transcripts - wider legal-hold scope, e-discovery cost, error propagation.
- Shadow AI: iManage benchmark (2026-02-11): 30% experienced policy-impacting incidents from unregulated AI tools.
- Market-structure risk to knowledge publishers: after Anthropic's legal plugin launch, Thomson Reuters fell 18%, RELX 14%, Wolters Kluwer 13% (Legal.io, 2026-03-06) - evidence that markets price proprietary content/knowledge as the contested asset.

## Distinct viewpoints found

1. KM-as-foundation (dominant): AI is commodity; curated, governed knowledge is the constraint and differentiator (Inside Practice, Benamram, LexisNexis, iManage, Harvey, Tiger Eye, ClearPeople).
2. Context-over-content: what matters is situational context delivered to the model - "context engineering" - not the size of the library (Weinberg, Walters).
3. Sceptical/measurement view: firms are buying AI but cannot show impact; vendor-sponsored "knowledge foundation" research is self-interested and methodologically thin (Embry, Above the Law; note most KM-and-AI statistics come from DMS/KM vendors).
4. Proprietary-data-moat view: incumbents' datasets and expertise are the defence against foundation-model entrants (Ambrogi), versus the market's reaction that the moat is weaker than thought (share-price falls).
5. Security-first view: opening knowledge to AI collides with ethical walls, privilege and retention; KM has to work harder, not looser (Tellyn, Humberstone, Hintyr, Mayer Brown, Debevoise).
6. In-house governance view: legal ops' near-term AI job is governance and operating model (CLOC, Harbor, Legal.io); the in-house frameworks (Core 12, ACC) have not yet re-described KM for AI.
7. RAG-is-not-enough view: even curated, RAG-based legal tools err roughly one time in five; human verification persists (Stanford RegLab; AI Law Librarians).
