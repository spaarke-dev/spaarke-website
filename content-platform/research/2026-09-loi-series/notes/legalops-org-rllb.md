### Thematic read of the 2026 agenda (my synthesis from the above; label as inference)
1. AI has moved from "adopt" to "prove": two of the confirmed items (Harvey/Exxon session; Kabiri workshop) are explicitly about measurement and ROI evidence.
2. Agentic AI framed around shipped outcomes, not architecture (Riehl).
3. Adoption and change management as the binding constraint ("The Adoption Paradox").
4. Outside counsel and ALSP management remains a core legal-ops staple, now with law-firm economics under AI pressure ("The Law Firm of 2030").
5. CLM being re-examined in light of AI ("CLM Is Dead. Long Live CLM!").
6. "Command center" / architecture language for the legal ops data layer (Brightflag session title; low confidence).
7. Heavy leadership and soft-skills programming persists, consistent with the Leadership and Personal Effectiveness reference model.
NOT FOUND: any confirmed 2026 session title on knowledge management, legal intake / legal front door, or self-service. They very likely exist among 100+ sessions (Checkbox AI is a Diamond sponsor), but I could not verify a single title.

## 6. Viewpoints observed

1. LegalOps.com founders: legal ops is a "platform for strategic leadership", needs a blueprint (reference models), credentialing, and the whole ecosystem (in-house, firms, providers) in one room. AI matters but sits alongside KM, planning, budgeting, right-sourcing, vendor management.
2. Vendor recap view (TCDI): AI conversation has matured from "whether" to "where and how to prove value"; people, process and adoption still decide outcomes.
3. AI-vendor view (Harvey): the next problem is measurement; usage analytics are the foundation of the ROI case.
4. Technologist view (Riehl, as reported): agents should target the high-volume middle of the legal-work bell curve and ship outcomes, not architecture.
5. Law-firm view (as reported): AI-lowered delivery cost could make flat fees more profitable; clients will expect tech-enabled service and predictable fees.
6. Risk/GRC vendor view (Exterro): legal as a "value-creating risk engine".
