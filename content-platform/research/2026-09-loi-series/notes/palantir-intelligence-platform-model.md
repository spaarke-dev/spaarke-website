## 1. The model, precisely

### 1.1 The core claim: model decisions, not just data

- The central system "represents the decisions in an enterprise, not simply the data." Each operational decision is modelled as four components: Data ("The information leveraged to make the decision"), Logic ("The heuristics and computational processes that evaluate a decision"), Action ("The orchestration and execution of the chosen decision"), Security ("The assurance that the decision complies with operational policies").
  Source: Palantir docs, "Why create an Ontology?" https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/why-ontology (undated, accessed 2026-09-21). Same text in blog "Connecting Agents to Decisions", 2026-04-28, https://blog.palantir.com/connecting-agents-to-decisions-277dee8ddb40
- "The Ontology models decisions through the four-fold integration of data, logic, action, and security."
  Source: docs, "The Ontology system" https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/architecture-center/ontology-system (undated, accessed 2026-09-21)
- Nouns and verbs: "The data objects, or "nouns", however, must be complemented by "verbs" in order to model decisions; semantics must be paired with kinetics." Same source.
- Operational vs analytical: "Closing the action loop as decisions are made in real-time is what distinguishes an operational system from an analytical system." Source: why-ontology docs; also the 2026-04-28 blog.

### 1.2 What it consolidates (all sources, in context)

- Sources named by Palantir: "structured data, streaming and edge sources, unstructured repositories, imagery data, and more" plus "the data generated by end users and agents as decisions are being made" (called "decision data"). ERPs, MES, WMS, IoT/edge, unstructured repositories, geospatial stores. Source: why-ontology docs.
- "Data can flow from every conceivable source, such as fragmented ERP estates, homegrown systems of record, CRMs, industrial databases, geospatial repositories, real-time sensors, document stores". Source: ontology-system docs.
- Models are consolidated too: forecast models, optimizers, business rules in CRMs/ERPs, ML models in data-science environments are bound in as "logic assets" through a "logic binding" paradigm, regardless of where they run. Source: why-ontology docs.
- Purpose of consolidation is context for people to understand, assess, act: the worked example runs through "Gaining visibility into the problem", "Building simulations and designing solutions", "Executing decisions and taking action", "Learning from decisions". Source: why-ontology docs. The 10-K describes Gotham as enabling users "to see, understand, and act". Source: Palantir FY2025 Form 10-K, signed 2026-02-17, https://investors.palantir.com/files/2025%20FY%20PLTR%2010-K.pdf
- The operational layer sits on top of integrated assets and ties them to real things: "The Palantir Ontology is an operational layer for the organization." It "sits on top of the digital assets integrated into the Palantir platform (datasets, virtual tables, and models) and connects them to their real-world counterparts". Source: https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/overview (undated, accessed 2026-09-21)

### 1.3 The three layers

Palantir's own wording for the three-part framing:
- "It integrates the semantic, kinetic, and dynamic elements of your business" (Foundry product page, https://www.palantir.com/platforms/foundry/, undated, accessed 2026-09-21).
- "a developer toolchain that surfaces the full power of the semantic data, dynamic logic, and kinetic action integrated into the Ontology" (AIP product page, https://www.palantir.com/platforms/aip/, undated, accessed 2026-09-21). This is the cleanest primary mapping: semantic = data, kinetic = action, dynamic = logic.
- Note: the current documentation mostly uses the newer four-part framing (data, logic, action, security) and keeps "semantic" and "kinetic" as adjectives. "Dynamic" appears in marketing copy more than in docs. The docs overview lists kinetic elements as "(actions, functions, dynamic security)".

**Semantic layer (what exists).** Entities, properties, relationships.
- "An object type is the schema definition of a real-world entity or event." https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/object-link-types/object-types-overview
- "A link type is the schema definition of a relationship between two object types." https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/object-link-types/link-types-overview
- A property is "the schema definition of a characteristic of a real-world entity or event." https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/core-concepts
- Interfaces give polymorphism: "An interface is an Ontology type that describes the shape of an object type and its capabilities." (core-concepts)
- Crucially the model is bound to real data: "Rather than being an abstract data model, the Foundry Ontology maps each ontological concept to an organization's actual data". An object type is "analogous to that of a dataset", an object to a row, a link type to a join. (object-types-overview, link-types-overview)
- "Defining the semantics of your organization happens by mapping existing datasources into objects, properties, and links". (ontology/overview)

**Kinetic layer (what can be done).** Actions, functions, writeback, process.
- "The kinetics of the organization—enabling change while complying with organizational controls and governance—are defined in the Ontology using action types and functions." (ontology/overview)
- "An action type is the definition of a set of changes or edits to objects, property values, and links that a user can take at once." It "also includes the side effect behaviors that occur with action submission." https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/action-types/overview
- An action is "a single transaction that changes the properties of one or more objects, based on a user-defined logic." Users think "about overall objectives instead of specific property edits." (action-types/overview)
- Rules and approvals: submission criteria "support encoding business logic into data editing permissions". They combine user, group, parameter and object state, e.g. only a flight controller may change an aircraft, and only to one still in operation. https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/action-types/submission-criteria
- Functions: "A function is a piece of code-based logic that takes in input parameters and returns an output." (core-concepts). Functions read properties, traverse links, make edits, call external systems and models. https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/functions/overview
- Writeback to source systems: a webhook lets an action send a request to "Salesforce, SAP, or any configured HTTP server". "This enables workflows in Foundry to connect directly with source systems and write back data and decisions into those systems." A "writeback" webhook runs before anything else; "if the webhook execution fails, no other changes will be made", giving "some degree of transactionality". https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/action-types/webhooks
- Writeback is tuned per receiving system in the worked example: WMS gets an API update, three ERPs get native connector updates, the planning system gets a flat file. (why-ontology docs)
- Process: marketing copy describes defining "business processes — including their tasks, transitions, and teams — in a first-class way" and linking actions to process models. https://www.palantir.com/explore/platforms/foundry/ontology/ (undated; text extracted from page data). 2022 blog: "Ontology Process Flows represent the decision-making flows throughout the organization, with embedded governance". https://blog.palantir.com/the-ontology-operating-at-optimum-complexity-as-simply-as-possible-39f4a9724fdf (2022-12-09, Markus Loeffler)

**Dynamic layer (what could happen; logic, models, simulation, AI).**
- Logic can be "a simple business rule, a conventional machine learning model, an LLM-driven function, or a complex multi-step orchestration". (ontology-system docs)
- Models bound to entities: "By incorporating ML models, the Ontology becomes a single source of truth for the organization, not just in terms of data, but also in terms of logic." This is what "unlocks the ability to simulate changes across the organization". https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/models
- Simulation: "A scenario is essentially a sandbox to apply edits on top of the data in your Ontology, generated by applying one or more actions." Uses: what-if analysis, business logic simulation, agent evaluation, comparative analysis; scenarios can be merged into live data. Marked [Beta]. https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/overview-ontology-scenario
- "Human and agent actions" can "be safely staged as scenarios, governed with the same granular access controls as data and logic primitives". (why-ontology docs)

### 1.4 Decisions captured as data and fed back

- "The data asset grows in richness and value as user decisions and insights are captured in the form of edits to the Ontology." (action-types/overview)
- "The action log is designed to capture decisions made by submitting actions and make these decisions available as data in the Ontology." It answers "What decision was made, by whom, when, and in what context?" https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/action-types/action-log
- "Decision lineage": "when a given decision was made, atop which version of enterprise data, and through which application, is automatically captured". (why-ontology docs)
- Learning loop: "Every piece of feedback gathered within a workflow can be securely incorporated into continuous learning loops, and used to power the journey from augmentation to automation." (ontology-system docs). Aggregate decisions "can be securely leveraged as training data when fine-tuning models, and can be distilled into targeted principles that are called upon during agent prompting." (why-ontology docs)
- The loop in plain words: "use data to make a decision, capture the decision that was made, and then use data to assess the decision's impact over time." https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/getting-started/delivering-a-use-case
- Use cases create "re-usable user interfaces that close the operational loop by capturing decisions as data". https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/use-case-life-cycle/overview
- 2022 framing: "Writeback captures the impact of actions, processes, and related data to ensure that the Ontology — and the actions it drives — steadily improve over time." "Data, analytics, and models created by technology are meaningless without connectivity to real-world action." (Loeffler blog, 2022-12-09)
- Independent corroboration (AWS partner blog, 2021-09-15, co-authored with Palantir staff, so not fully independent): "Any decision, and the context in which it was taken, will be written back to the ontology and, if applicable, the original data source." https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/apn/how-palantir-foundry-helps-customers-build-and-deploy-ai-powered-decision-making-applications/

### 1.5 Digital twin

- "the Ontology serves as a digital twin of the organization, containing both the semantic elements (objects, properties, links) and kinetic elements (actions, functions, dynamic security)". (ontology/overview)
- "The Ontology serves as "the digital twin of an organization"" wording also in core-concepts. Getting-started calls it "a digital representation of your organization". https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/getting-started/introductory-concepts
- Use case lifecycle: "structuring a flexible, re-usable data asset as a digital twin of our organization and business processes". (use-case-life-cycle/overview)

## 2. Organization structure, roles, responsibilities, permissions, staff assignments; security travelling with data

### 2.1 People and structure are modelled as ordinary entities, relationships and actions

- The documentation's own running example is an Employee entity. "Links can also exist between two objects of the same type. A link type Direct Report ↔ Manager can be defined between the Employee object type and itself." (link-types-overview). Employee → Employer (Company) link is the other example.
- Staff assignment is an action: "You may create an Assign Employee action type that defines how users can change the role property value for a given Employee object." It can create a link to the new Manager, "notify the old and new manager of the change", and "Validate that authorized employees such as those working in human resources can perform the action." (action-types/overview)
- Process teams: processes are defined with "their tasks, transitions, and teams". (explore ontology page)
- Work routing to responsible staff is a standard application pattern: "Inboxes are commonly used to enable operational users to triage, prioritize and complete tasks." https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/workshop/overview. The requirement template ties user type to decision: "A route operations analyst (user type) reviews a flight alert inbox (interface) for their responsible routes and triages alerts (decision)... to re-assign, resolve, or escalate (action)". https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/use-case-life-cycle/distilling-functional-requirements
- Inference (mine, not stated by Palantir): the platform has no separate "org chart module". Organization structure is just part of the semantic model (Person, Team, Role entities; reports-to, member-of, responsible-for relationships), and assignment and delegation are governed actions. Platform identity (users, groups, organizations from the identity provider) is a separate security construct; see 2.2.

### 2.2 Permissions: layered, and partly mandatory

- Two families: "Data security in the Palantir platform is provided through a combination of mandatory and discretionary controls, which differ in how they propagate." https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/security/overview
- Discretionary: roles (Owner, Editor, Viewer, Discoverer) granted on projects, usually to groups; groups may come from an external identity provider. https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/security/projects-and-roles
- Mandatory: markings, classification-based controls, organizations. "Organizations are one form of mandatory controls applied to Projects that enforce strict silos between groups of users and resources." (security/overview)
- Row, column, cell security on entities independent of source permissions: "Object security policies allow you to configure view permissions on an object instance... These are used to achieve row-level security." Property policies give column-level; combined give cell-level. https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/object-permissioning/object-security-policies
- Policies are computed per interaction: "These policies are dynamically computed at runtime for every interaction", combining row and column restrictions, user-group attributes "including those that flow via SSO", and markings. (why-ontology docs)
- Schema vs data permissions are separate: "ontology resources" (the type definitions) and "objects and links" (the data) are permissioned on two levels. https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/object-permissioning/overview
- Who may act: action permissions answer "Who can apply an action type with a given set of parameters?"; user must see the edited entities and "pass the submission criteria". https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/action-types/permissions
- Different scopes per capability: "The ability to trigger a purchase order might have granular permissions, while the ability to run a scenario... might be more permissible". (ontology-system docs)
- Collaborative segregation of duties: a model "that segments those who can explore possible decisions, those who can stage decisions for review, and those who can commit those decisions". (blog 2026-04-28)

### 2.3 Security and governance travel with the data

- "Unlike role-based access, which is based on where data lives in the platform, Markings travel with the data." "Markings are inherited along both the file hierarchy and direct dependencies and propagate through transform and analysis logic." https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/security/markings
- "Mandatory controls (markings, Classification-based Access Controls, and organizations) travel with each unit of data through derivation, via Palantir's provenance and lineage capabilities." (security/overview)
- Important nuance Palantir itself flags: row and column controls are "Read-time enforcement only"; they "do not extend to downstream outputs or exports", so they must be paired with a mandatory control. https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/security/access-control-propagation
- Application builders inherit security: "application builders are not required to account for these security requirements as they are resident in the ontology itself". https://blog.palantir.com/ontology-finding-meaning-in-data-palantir-rfx-blog-series-1-399bd1a5971b (2022-10-03)
- Purpose justification: "Checkpoints is a data governance tool that supports purpose justification and auditability." Users are prompted to justify sensitive interactions; records are reviewable. https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/checkpoints/overview
- Approvals: change requests are routed for approval and "persisted even if they have been completed, so you can reference them as an audit log of past decisions." https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/approvals/overview
- Lineage: "Every dataset in the Palantir platform maintains a record of how it was produced". (introductory-concepts)
- Auditability (2022): "users with appropriate permissions have access to the complete history of all data that enters the system and all user actions". (Loeffler blog)

## 3. How it differs from a warehouse, a lake, and BI dashboards (their words)

- vs analytics architectures generally: "Traditional data architectures do not capture the reasoning that goes into decision-making or the actions that follow, and therefore limit learning and the incorporation of AI." "Conventional analytics architectures do not contextualize computation in lived reality, and remain disconnected from operations." (why-ontology docs)
- vs a reporting database: integrating enterprise data plus decision data "requires a different architecture than a classical database management solution optimized for reporting and analytics." (why-ontology docs)
- vs curated warehouse tables: "Instead of dealing with golden tables that flatten the richness of operations into narrow schemas, the full expanse of the enterprise comes to life in the form of objects, properties, and links". (blog 2026-04-28)
- Customer voice Palantir reports: they had "brought data together, created golden tables, created risk models, visualized." and said "I got all my data in one place. But it’s not helping me when I need it most." https://blog.palantir.com/the-ontology-resilience-in-crisis-7833bb5e15e7 (2022-12-04)
- vs a catalog or schema tool: "Far beyond data cataloging or schema design solutions, the Ontology allows you to define a robust foundation for end-user workflows". (ontology/overview)
- vs a semantic layer: "The Ontology is not a "semantic layer"; the fourfold integration and operationalization of data, logic, action, and security cannot be accomplished with a thin semantic layer or a monolithic design." (ontology-system docs)
- vs dashboards: applications are "user-driven workflows that go far beyond dashboards or passive visualizations." https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/app-building/overview. "Applications built in Workshop are more dynamic and interactive than typical dashboards created in other point-and-click tools." (workshop/overview). Guidance: "instead of starting with a need to build a sales dashboard, seek to understand what decisions and outcomes your work might enable." (delivering-a-use-case)
- vs RAG-style AI: "The Ontology enables agents to go beyond the data-centric limitations of retrieval-augmented generation". (why-ontology docs)
- It coexists with lakes and warehouses rather than replacing them: the data plane is meant to be "deeply integrated with existing data lakes, lakehouses, conventional warehouses, data governance tools". https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/architecture-center/multimodal-data-plane. Virtual tables let Databricks, Snowflake, BigQuery assets be used "without needless data duplication". https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/architecture-center/interoperability
- 2022 structural definition: "The Ontology service must separate data pipelines and applications." "New data only needs to be mapped to one place (the ontology) and new applications can be built leveraging the existing object logic." (RFx blog 2022-10-03)
- Summary in neutral words: a warehouse or lake stores and serves data for analysis; BI shows it. This model adds (a) a shared business-entity model bound to live data, (b) governed actions that write back to operational systems, (c) bound logic and models, (d) capture of each decision as new data. The distinguishing test is whether the loop from insight to action to recorded outcome is closed inside one governed system.

## 4. How such a platform is built, step by step

Reconstructed from Palantir's use-case lifecycle, ontology design guidance, and blog material.

1. **Start from an operational decision, not from data or a dashboard.** "A use case is a time-bound effort by a dedicated team to support a specific decision-making process." (delivering-a-use-case). "A use case is not "integrate source system X" or "apply ML technique Y"." (use-case-life-cycle/overview). Foundry was built "backwards, starting from the most critical operational decisions". (Foundry product page)
2. **Write the requirement as a decision sentence.** Template: "[User Type] [Interface] [Decision] [Decision Inputs] [Action]". Also capture: "What is the ultimate decision being made? What are the intermediate decisions? Which users go with which decisions?" and outcomes and KPIs. (distilling-functional-requirements)
3. **Model the entities the decision needs, in business language.** "Model reality, not systems: Object types should represent real-world entities, not individual source system or department representations." Priority principles: domain-driven design; do not repeat yourself; open for extension, closed for modification; composition over deep hierarchies. Anti-pattern: mapping source columns 1:1 (the "Kitchen Sink"). https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/ontology-best-practices
4. **Bind to existing systems; do not replace them.** Map "existing datasources" into entities (ontology/overview); use connectors for batch, micro-batch, streaming https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/data-integration/overview; use virtual tables so data "never requires data to be duplicated" (multimodal-data-plane). "Federation ensures that the Ontology can harness the full power of your enterprise architecture, without fracturing existing sources of truth." (explore ontology page). On ERPs: "The ERP becomes what it always should have been: the system of record for accountability, not the system of constraint for operations." https://blog.palantir.com/enterprise-business-software-and-the-mixed-up-chameleon-problem-f16df333bc71 (2026-05-29)
5. **Define actions with rules, validations, approvals, side effects, writeback.** "Choose the right tool: Use action types for human or agentic decisions and pipelines for automated transformations." (ontology-best-practices). Submission criteria, notifications, webhooks, action log (see 1.3, 1.4).
6. **Build workflow applications on the entity layer.** Patterns: task and alert inboxes, common operational pictures, object views. Applications read entities, use actions for writeback and functions for logic. (workshop/overview)
7. **Sequence: data asset and guided decisions first, models and AI second.** "first establishing a data asset (1) and guided decision-making (2)... You can then proceed into decision space expansion and enrichment, incorporating data science techniques for model-driven operational workflows." Foundations: robust pipeline; "a balanced, reflective, and practical object model"; enrichment; "re-usable interface components for object views and decision capture." https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/use-case-life-cycle/sequencing-development
8. **Add AI on top, with tools drawn from the same model** (section 5).
9. **Validate against real questions.** "Real business questions are the best acceptance tests". Derive them from "What do leaders repeatedly ask their teams?" Test with new users and with agents; "Time to answer is a design signal". https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/ontology-design-validation
10. **Iterate and expand; value compounds.** "new applications can take advantage of the preexisting system integration work", because translation into the shared model happens "once, rather than every time a new application is built." https://blog.palantir.com/ontology-oriented-software-development-68d7353fdb12 (2024-01-22, Peter Wilczynski). "it allows for the data ecosystem to grow and evolve over time in ways that create compounding value rather than ever increasing complexity." (RFx blog 2022-10-03)

**Delivery approach (bootcamp, forward deployed engineering).**
- "Palantir’s AIP Bootcamp is an interactive workshop where customers go from 0 to use case in 5 days." Three aims: understand how to apply AI to operations; "Develop initial use cases in the software"; "Onboard and train users for rollout". https://www.palantir.com/platforms/aip/bootcamp/ (undated, accessed 2026-09-21). Getting-started docs now say "from zero to use case in hours or days". April 2026 blog uses a newer name, "AIP AgentCamps", "achieving outcomes with AI in a matter of hours".
- 10-K: "our use of AIP bootcamps, which allow us to deliver real workflows on actual customer data in days." Risk factor: the prior model "required us to spend months" on low-cost pilots; bootcamps are "shorter, more cost-effective programs" that "may result in no or minimal future revenue." (FY2025 10-K, 2026-02-17)
- Partner description of format (secondary, Palantir reseller PVM, 2024-07-24): prep with static data cuts one to two weeks ahead and a 30-minute ontology discussion with data owners; then demos, hands-on-keyboard build, showcase and next steps. https://blog.pvmit.com/pvm-blog/palantir-platform-bootcamp-guide
- Forward deployed engineering: "This is the human equivalent of backpropagation, in which teams of engineers get as close as possible to a problem while working in concert with core engineering teams". https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/architecture-center/overview. There is now an "AI FDE", "an interactive agent that operates Foundry for you through conversational commands". https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ai-fde/overview

## 5. How AI and LLM agents use the model

- **Grounding.** "The Ontology integrates real-time data from all relevant sources into a semantic model of the business." This "anchors AI in the operational truth of the enterprise, mitigating the risk of model hallucinations". (AIP product page; same sentence in blog 2023-12-05 https://blog.palantir.com/building-with-palantir-aip-data-tools-for-rag-oag-b3b509c8b0f3)
- **Beyond retrieval.** "OAG is a more expansive, decision-centric version of Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG)." It grounds LLMs "via the decision-centric Ontology, which brings together the three constituent elements of decision-making — data, logic, and actions". https://blog.palantir.com/building-with-palantir-aip-logic-tools-for-rag-oag-fdaf8938d02e (2024-01-31)
- **Tools come from the model.** Agent tool types: Action ("Gives your chatbot the ability to execute an ontology edit. This can be configured to run automatically or to run after confirmation from the user."), Object query (filter, aggregate, traverse links, restricted to named entity types and properties), Function (any function, including LLM-backed ones), plus request-clarification. https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/agent-studio/tools
- **Deterministic logic complements LLM reasoning.** "Deterministic functions, algorithms, and conventional statistical processes can serve as operational tools which complement the non-deterministic reasoning of LLMs". (why-ontology docs). LLMs are "not great at traditional types of computation, like forecasting or linear optimization". (blog 2024-01-31)
- **Same security as humans.** "all agentic activity is controlled with the same security policies that govern human usage". Tool invocations "are dependent on access to the underlying objects, properties, and links". Agent permissions "either inherit from a human user, or from the permissions structure of a defined project." (why-ontology; ontology-system docs). "These platform security controls grant an LLM access only to what is necessary to complete a task." https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/logic/overview
- **Human in the loop by default; autonomy is earned.** "With the consequences of the plan safely staged in a scenario, the agent’s proposed decision is handed off to a human analyst for final review." By default actions "can only be staged by the AI". The organization can choose "whether any trusted, well-tested AI processes can automatically close the action loop without human review", and "the latitude given to AI can be expanded or contracted". "Each constructed and deployed agent can be treated like a new team member that is gradually granted a wider purview". (why-ontology docs). "Logic functions can also be automated so that Ontology edits can be automatically applied or staged for human review." (logic/overview)
- **Observability and evaluation.** "logging for every action taken by human users or AI agents"; evaluation framework with test cases and cross-model comparison. https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/architecture-center/aip-architecture
- **Learning.** Decision lineage feeds fine-tuning and prompt principles; agent memory (working, episodic, semantic, procedural) is refined from decision data. (blog 2026-04-28)
- **Design implication.** The entity model is the interface for agents too: a good model lets "a user, or an AI agent... navigate it without friction, because the structure matches how they already think about their domain." (ontology-best-practices). "People and agents expose different weaknesses". (ontology-design-validation)
- Naming note: "AIP Chatbot Studio was previously known as AIP Agent Studio, and AIP Chatbots were previously known as AIP Agents." https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/agent-studio/overview

## 6. Translation table (proprietary term to safe neutral term)

Trademark evidence found: the FY2025 10-K says Palantir has "registered Palantir as a trademark" and has "registered trademarks for Gotham, Palantir Foundry, and our corporate logo". ## 8. Viewpoints and disagreements

- **Palantir's own position has shifted.** 2022: ontology as "systematic mapping of data to meaningful semantic concepts", a layer separating pipelines from applications. 2024 to 2026: "decision-centric", four-fold data/logic/action/security, explicitly "not a semantic layer", and framed around human-agent teaming. An article should follow the later framing.
- **Independent explainers often get the layers wrong.** Cristian Caruso (Medium, 2025-06-13) defines the kinetic layer as mapping raw data and ETL into the model, and the dynamic layer as business rules, access control and lifecycle. That contradicts Palantir's own usage (kinetic = actions/functions/writeback; dynamic = logic/models/simulation). https://pythonebasta.medium.com/understanding-palantirs-ontology-semantic-kinetic-and-dynamic-layers-explained-c1c25b39ea3c. Reason to avoid the three-word triad entirely.
- **Competitor critique (vendor source, Elementum, 2026-07-16):** the model "still needs its own indexed copy of the data, regardless of whose cloud that copy sits in", and mapping source systems into entity types is "specialized, judgment-heavy work" usually led by the vendor's engineers; lock-in and staffing dependency follow. https://www.elementum.ai/blog/palantir-ontology-explained. Palantir's counter-position is virtual tables and "never requires data to be duplicated" (multimodal-data-plane docs). These two claims are in tension; the truth likely depends on workload (indexing for operational query vs virtualized analytical access). Not resolved here.
- **Trade press (Glitchwire, 2026-04-28, byline "AI Desk"):** largely restates Palantir's April 2026 post; notes switching costs once an organization's operating model is encoded in one vendor's platform. "The Ontology models actions as first-class primitives, with the same rigorous access controls and audit trails that govern data access." https://glitchwire.com/news/palantir-ontology-the-architecture-that-turns-ai-agents-into-decision-making-sys/
- **Partner view (Cognizant, 2025-03-25):** "the digital twin of an organization, a rich semantic layer that sits on top of digital assets" (quote as returned by fetch tool, not verified against raw HTML). Uses "semantic layer", the label Palantir now rejects. https://www.cognizant.com/us/en/the-power-of-ontology-in-palantir-foundry
- **Palantir's own candour on limits:** row and column controls are read-time only and do not propagate; scenarios are beta; bootcamps "may result in no or minimal future revenue" (10-K).
