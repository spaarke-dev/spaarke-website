## Driver 1: a new viewpoint on resource requirements, driven by AI

The library supports this driver as the primary one. Three association or consultancy surveys published between December 2025 and May 2026 say the same thing from different angles: demand is rising, budgets and headcount are flat, AI is the assumed answer, and the operating model (roles, workflows, governance, measurement) has not caught up. The strongest usable statements that AI is changing how departments think about staffing and sourcing are ACC's "AI Adoption Targets Role Evolution Over Headcount Reduction" heading, Harbor's "Technology is in place. The operating model has not kept up.", Deloitte US on non-traditional roles, and the ACC/Everlaw and Blickstein insourcing intentions.

## Driver 2: legal, and particularly risk, recognised as a critical business consideration

The library's evidence for this driver is thinner and more mixed than for Driver 1, and the writer should know that the strongest single datapoint cuts both ways: GCs believe legal is a significant contributor (86%) but only 17% of their C-suite peers agree. The library reads that gap as a visibility problem, which fits an article whose thesis is that legal operations must produce the evidence. The direct statements that risk evaluation is moving to the business are Deloitte US (a recommendation, not a prediction) and KPMG (an extracted quote, not verified). No board-level or CEO-level survey exists in the library; the closest is TR's separate C-suite survey (sample size not given) and Axiom's CFO-inclusive budgeting survey.

## Gaps and cautions for the writer

1. Three writer-named figures are outside the date window: Deloitte UK 84% and 72%-to-44% (2026-07-09), Axiom 7% and 83% (2026-06-29 / 2026-07-09), Gartner 56% headcount freeze (2026-07-28 reprint). In-window substitutes: Harbor 2026-05-11 ("Technology is in place. The operating model has not kept up."), ACC 63% stable headcount with role evolution, Axiom 2026-02-17 GC survey (over 80% plan to reallocate law firm work), Axiom 2025-09-08 budgeting survey (78% mandated to implement AI without dedicated budgets).
2. The Deloitte US "2026 Predictions for Chief Legal Officers" PDF carries a 2026 copyright but no publication date in the library. It is almost certainly a start-of-year document, but the writer should confirm the date (or cite with an access date) before relying on it for a 2026-06-14 article.
3. The KPMG 2026 Global General Counsel Outlook has no publication date in the library and none of its figures or quotes were fact-checked (the checker could not reach KPMG). Fieldwork ended February 2026. If the writer wants the 75% "advise on non-legal business issues" figure or the Athanasoulas quote, a date check and a verbatim check are both needed.
4. No board-level or CEO-level survey exists in the library. The closest items are TR's C-suite comparison (17%; C-suite sample size not stated), ACC's CLO-reported board attendance (79%), and Axiom's CFO-inclusive budgeting survey (18-point authority gap). A CEO or board survey on legal risk would need new research.
5. Gartner on "legal departments and business risk": the library has only the 2025-10-01 GC priorities survey (104 GCs; 36% AI, 9% contract analytics) from a secondary reprint, and a 2025-12-17 release known by title only. Neither states that legal or risk is recognised as critical to the business.
6. EY 2025 figures: the data-quality set (52%, 44%, 41%, 75%) was writer-verified in May 2026; the budget and operating-model set (83%, 87%, 11%, 20%, 60%) rests on search snippets and was never fetched.
7. The ACC/MLA 2026 Benchmarking Report is dated only by its file path (2026-06), so it cannot be safely placed before 2026-06-14; its first dated coverage is 2026-07-09. Its copyright also requires "Reprinted with permission" wording on extracts.
8. Items recorded through the summarizing fetch tool (marked EXTRACTED) and not seen verbatim by a fact-checker: Clem "paradox" quote (Lexpert), Brown quote (Lexpert), both KPMG quotes, Snell 2026-03-10 blog wording, DiLuca quotes, the "central authority for non-financial risk" phrase attributed to ACC. The writer has waived the fact-check, but these should not be printed inside quotation marks without a verbatim check.
9. Em dashes: several recorded quotes (ABA Journal Franke, CCBJ Franke and standfirst) contain em dashes in the original. The house rule means these must be paraphrased or the dash elided with an ellipsis or restructured sentence.
