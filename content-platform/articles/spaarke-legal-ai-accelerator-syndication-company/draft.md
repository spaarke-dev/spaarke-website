Microsoft is now the platform for legal AI.

Most legal teams have run proofs of concept with Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, and specialized legal tools. Those projects showed where AI fits in legal work. But they sit outside the core legal systems and run under each vendor's own controls, which has kept most of the work with individual lawyers and pilot groups.

Since October, Microsoft Copilot carries legal skills in Word and Cowork. They replace or augment Claude for Legal, Harvey, Legora, and other legal AI tools, from review and redlining in Word to multi-document analysis in Cowork. All of it runs inside the organization's own tenant.

What sets the platform apart is that each legal team can tune it. Plugins, playbooks, and MCP connectors customize Copilot to the team's own procedures, precedents, and protocols, so it works the way the team and each practitioner work. Documents stay in the tenant, the organization's existing governance applies, and the team's playbooks remain its own intellectual capital.

The Spaarke Legal AI Accelerator for Microsoft Copilot puts that platform to work, one practice in one week. It combines core legal skills, practice packs, calculation tools for the work that must be exact, and forward-deployed legal engineers who configure all of it to the team's practice.

In a new article, our founder Ralph Schroeder sets out why Microsoft is now the platform for legal AI, how legal AI on Microsoft is built, and how everything the accelerator builds carries forward onto the Legal Operations Intelligence platform.
