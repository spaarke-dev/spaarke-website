Every legal team is now deciding where its legal AI will live. My answer is Microsoft, and my new article sets out why.

The early work with Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, and specialized legal tools taught legal teams a great deal about where AI fits. It did not give them a platform the whole legal organization can build on, one connected to the matters, documents, and spend in their core systems and governed by their own controls.

Microsoft now provides that platform. Copilot runs Claude, GPT, and Microsoft's own models inside the organization's tenant, carries legal skills in Word and Cowork, and lets each team tune those skills to its own playbooks, precedents, and protocols through plugins and MCP connectors.

Tuning it well takes legal expertise as much as technical skill. Someone has to know which positions the team holds firm, which it will trade, and how an experienced lawyer would review the same document. That is why we built the Spaarke Legal AI Accelerator for Microsoft Copilot around forward-deployed legal engineers as well as pre-built skills and tools. A first engagement covers one practice in one week, and everything it builds stays the team's own.
