# Topic

A founder long-form article announcing the Spaarke Legal AI Accelerator for
Microsoft Copilot: why Microsoft's enterprise AI platform lets legal teams
build the legal AI they need, what the accelerator provides and how a legal
team starts with it, and how it builds toward and onto the Spaarke Legal
Operations Intelligence platform. Hero asset of the campaign
`2026-11-legal-ai-accelerator`, which runs in the paid LinkedIn program.

# Angle / Point of view

Thesis sentence for the opening (draft wording; the writer fixes it at the
plan gate):

> Microsoft now provides an enterprise AI platform on which legal teams can
> build the legal AI they need, inside the Microsoft environment they already
> govern, and the Spaarke Legal AI Accelerator for Microsoft Copilot gives
> them the pre-built skills, tools, and connectors, and the forward-deployed
> legal engineers, to put that platform to work in days.

**Thesis formula (the fixed refrain):** "the killer legal AI platform",
carried in the title. It appears almost verbatim at three anchors: the
opening thesis, the heading of the section that proves it, and the close
(`ai-tells.md`, section 3.7). "Killer" is the piece's one superlative
(`style-guide.md`, section 5, rule 9), and the section that proves it has
to earn it with sourced facts.

**Required sections (writer, 2026-11-02):** one section on why Microsoft is
the killer legal AI platform; one section setting out the build, layer by
layer (enterprise governance with Entra ID and Purview; Microsoft 365,
SharePoint, Teams, Outlook, Power Apps and Dataverse; the legal platform's
systems of record for matter and case management, document management, and
finance, budgeting, and e-billing; the decision support ontology; legal
AI), with its own exhibit; one section on the accelerator, its structure,
and its components; and a maturity-path section that ties each artifact the
accelerator builds explicitly to those systems of record and the ontology.
The outline is in `plan.md`.

The argument in four steps:

1. **Microsoft has built the platform.** Microsoft 365 Copilot and Cowork are
   now a platform on which an organization builds its own AI: skills, agents,
   and connectors, packaged once and deployed across Copilot Chat, Cowork,
   and the Office apps. Microsoft's own legal skills in Word and its Legal for
   Cowork plugin show what the platform can do for legal work; they are a
   starting point, and the larger point is that legal teams can now build the
   AI they need on the same foundation.
2. **Four properties make it the right place for legal AI.** It is embedded in
   the work environment and connected to the data across it (mail, files,
   meetings, chats); it runs in the organization's own tenant and under its
   control; it is governed by the enterprise controls that already govern the
   organization's data; and it runs Claude models and OpenAI's GPT models,
   and is built for the models to come, including the frontier models
   Microsoft has announced for enterprise work. Usage runs on
   the organization's enterprise agreement and policies, which is a practical
   difference for users who hit the limits of individual AI subscriptions
   (see Voice notes on cost).
3. **The accelerator puts the platform to work.** A capable, flexible
   platform still has to be configured for legal work: which skills, which
   positions, which connectors, which tests. The Spaarke Legal AI Accelerator
   is a rapid deployment that provides pre-built legal skills, tools, and
   connectors, and forward-deployed legal engineers who bring the legal and
   technical expertise. Legal teams can deploy it themselves or with the
   engineers, and use it to start an AI program or to extend one already
   under way.
4. **It is the first step on the Legal Operations Intelligence maturity
   path.** What the accelerator builds is the legal team's own: its
   positions, its playbooks, its knowledge library, its evaluations. Each
   carries forward onto the Spaarke platform, which adds the matter, the
   policy, and the decision record (Exhibit 2).

Microsoft is presented as the most capable enterprise platform for legal AI,
never as limited. The accelerator's value is expertise, speed, and pre-built
legal content on that platform.

The competitive contrast is structural and unnamed: a separate legal AI
application runs in the vendor's environment, under the vendor's governance,
on the vendor's choice of model; legal AI built on Microsoft runs inside the
organization's own environment, tenant, governance, and model choice. The
article never names a competitor and never characterizes one beyond that
structure.

# Prime mover

Microsoft's enterprise AI platform is the force: AI that connects users to
the data across the tools they work in, runs leading models under enterprise
governance, and lets organizations build their own skills, agents, and
connectors. The opening sentence states the change; the second names this
force (`stance.md`, rule 3). The pressure on legal teams to show AI results,
the choice between a separate application and the organization's own
environment, and the maturity path are all subordinate to it. The four
properties in Angle step 2 are properties of the force, not coequal drivers,
and they appear as one bulleted list inside the section that explains why
the platform matters.

# The concession

Confirmed by the writer on 2026-10-30: the article names challenges legal
teams face, never failings of the reader. The challenge:

> Legal teams are being asked to show results from AI faster than they can
> build the capability to deliver them. The platform offers skills, agents,
> connectors, and a choice of models, and turning that into AI that does a
> practice's work the way the team does it takes legal and technical
> expertise that few legal teams have on staff.

The remedy, the accelerator's pre-built components and forward-deployed legal
engineers, follows in the same section.

**Stance rule 9, both claims.** The macro claim: AI is now part of the
environment where legal work is done, and legal operating models are being
rebuilt around it. The tool claim, stated in terms of what the accelerator
includes: skills tell the AI how to do a piece of legal work, and some parts
of that work must be exact every time, such as the notice deadline in a lease
or the arithmetic on an invoice. A model asked to compute those can return a
different answer on a different run, so the accelerator does that work with
tools, connected to Copilot through MCP, that calculate the same way on every
run. This is the reason the accelerator includes tools as well as skills,
and the article makes the point once, in the section on what is included.

# What we assert from practice

The writer holds these without a citation; the writer confirms the wording
at the plan gate:

- Every legal team is under pressure to settle its AI strategy and show
  results from it.
- Microsoft now provides the most capable platform for legal AI that runs
  inside the enterprise environment, and the platform, more than any single
  legal plugin, is what changes the situation for legal teams.
- Users of individual AI subscriptions tell us they run out of capacity
  ("I've run out of tokens"). Legal AI built on the organization's Microsoft
  agreement is provisioned and managed by the organization.
- Turning the platform into legal AI that does a practice's work takes
  legal and technical expertise that few legal teams have on staff.
- With pre-built components and forward-deployed legal engineers, a legal
  team can have its first practice's legal AI working in days.
- Work that must return the same answer every time belongs in a tool, not a
  prompt.

# Where the reader starts

The close names these, as imperatives addressed to legal operations leaders
(the plan may cut to the strongest four):

- Choose one practice and two or three recurring tasks where the team wants
  results first.
- Write down the team's positions for those tasks, with fallbacks and
  escalation, so they can be built into the skills.
- Gather the AI work already under way in the team, so the first deployment
  builds on it.
- Confirm the Microsoft set-up with IT: Copilot licences, the Cowork usage
  policy, and who approves connectors.
- Name the owner of each position once it is in the platform.

# Why now

Every legal team is under pressure to settle its AI strategy and approach and
to demonstrate results. Microsoft's enterprise AI platform makes it possible
to build legal AI inside the environment the organization already governs,
and the accelerator shortens the time from that decision to working results
from months to days.

position on record before Microsoft Ignite (17 to 20 November).

  - in the organization's own tenant and under its control;
  - governed by the enterprise controls that already govern the
    organization's data;
  - a choice of models: Claude models and OpenAI's GPT models today, and the
    models to come, including the frontier models Microsoft has announced
    for enterprise work.
- **Microsoft's legal skills as the starting point.** Name the legal drafting
  and review skills in Word and the Legal for Cowork plugin, as evidence of
  what the platform can do for legal work. The accelerator delivers its own
  skills on the same platform; the article does not describe it as an add-on
  to Microsoft's plugin.
- **The concession** (above), early, with its mechanism.
- **What's included**, as a list inside the section that proves the thesis,
  naming the technical components, the three content layers, and where
  Spaarke's expert content sits:
  - **Legal skills.** Packaged in the open Agent Skills format and deployed
    as plugins to Cowork and Copilot Chat. Foundation skills every practice
    uses (review against a playbook, build a playbook, issues list, key-terms
    extraction, document comparison, chronology, matter brief, citation and
    verification discipline) and specialty skills in each practice pack.
  - **Playbooks for Word.** Playbooks rendered as Word documents for Word's
    legal skills, built from the team's own positions.
  - **Knowledge library.** A SharePoint site structure for precedents, clause
    libraries, and checklists, in the organization's own tenant.
  - **Agents.** Copilot agents for a practice, grounded on the knowledge
    library and shared with the team.
  - **Tools and MCP connectors.** Calculation tools for work that must be
    exact (deadlines, rent schedules, patent terms, invoice arithmetic),
    connected through MCP, and configuration of the connectors to systems
    the organization already licenses, such as its document management
    system.
  - **Setup interview.** A guided skill that captures the team's positions,
    fallbacks, escalation contacts, and house style into files the
    organization owns.
  - **Evaluation set.** Questions and documents for each practice, extended
    with the team's own, so the team can test results against its current
    way of working.
  - **Governance and deployment.** Human-review guidance in every skill, an
    IT summary of what is installed and how data flows, an admin deployment
    package, and an auditable install.
  - **Adoption.** Installation guides, training prompts, and a usage
    baseline.
  The content comes in three layers, described in plain words and not as
  product names (writer, 2026-11-02): **the foundation** of core legal
  skills, the legal skills, governance, and
  evaluation that every practice uses (Spaarke-authored and maintained);
  **practice packs** of specialty skills, with expert tuning by Spaarke
  practitioners for
  Commercial Agreements, Transactions, Real Estate, Litigation, and
  Intellectual Property, plus an **Invoice Review** pack for legal
  operations (Spaarke-authored and maintained); and **the team's own
  playbooks**, its positions, fallbacks, and escalation rules, captured by
  the setup interview and never overwritten when Spaarke updates the other
  two layers.
- **Two ways to start.** Self-deployed: an attorney adds a practice pack in
  Cowork and shares it with colleagues, and a practice team adds the shared
  knowledge library and agent. With forward-deployed legal engineers: a
  one-week engagement that runs the Spaarke Method once, for one practice
  (envision, design and build, test on the team's own documents against a
  timed baseline, deploy), including admin deployment for organizations that
  install centrally. Define "forward-deployed legal engineer" in plain words
  on first use.
- **Start or extend.** The accelerator serves a team starting its AI program
  and a team extending one: it builds on skills, prompts, and documents the
  team already uses rather than replacing them.
- **Exhibit 1, the build (required).** A layered diagram, bottom to top:
  enterprise governance; Microsoft 365 and Power Platform; the legal
  platform (systems of record and the decision support ontology); legal AI.
  See `plan.md`, section 2.
- **Exhibit 2, the maturity path (required).** Shows how the accelerator

  individual; the team's legal AI with the accelerator; the Spaarke
