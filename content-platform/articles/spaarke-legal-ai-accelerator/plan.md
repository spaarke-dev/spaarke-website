## Title and article name

**Title (decided 2026-11-02):** Why Microsoft Is the Killer Legal AI
Platform

The options that were considered:

1. **Why Microsoft Is the Killer Legal AI Platform** (recommended). A
   statement, carries the refrain, and the description and summary
   introduce the accelerator.
2. **Microsoft Is the Killer Legal AI Platform** (the same claim, flatter).
3. **The Killer Legal AI Platform Is Already in Your Tenant** (sharper
   hook; leans on the tenant argument).
4. **Microsoft Is the Killer Legal AI Platform, and Spaarke Puts It to Work
   in Days** (names both halves; long for a card).

"What's the Killer Legal AI Platform: Microsoft" is not recommended: a colon
that stages a one-word answer is one of the constructions readers take as
machine-written (`ai-tells.md`, section 2.7).

"Killer" is informal for this register, and it is the piece's one
superlative. It works because the section under it proves it with sourced
facts.

**Article URL:** `/why-spaarke/microsoft-killer-legal-ai-platform`
(proposed; the workspace folder keeps the slug
`spaarke-legal-ai-accelerator`).

## Spine

> Microsoft is now the killer legal AI platform: it lets legal teams build
> the legal AI they need inside the environment they already govern,
> connected to their work across Microsoft 365, under their own governance,
> and on the models they choose. A platform this capable still has to be
> configured for legal work, and that takes legal and technical expertise
> few legal teams have on staff. The Spaarke Legal AI Accelerator for
> Microsoft Copilot supplies it: core legal skills, specialty skills for
> each practice, the tools and connectors, and forward-deployed legal
> engineers, so a team can start or extend its legal AI in days. Everything
> it builds belongs to the team and carries forward onto the Legal
> Operations Intelligence platform.

### Section 1: Microsoft is the killer legal AI platform

*The required section on why Microsoft.*

- **Claim:** Microsoft 365 Copilot and Cowork are now a platform for
  building legal AI, and four properties make it the right place for it.
- **Evidence:**
  - The platform: skills, agents, and connectors, packaged once and
    deployed across Copilot Chat, Cowork, and the Office apps (plugin
    registry, 2026-09-30). Cowork generally available since 2026-06-16.
  - Microsoft's own legal skills as the demonstration: the legal drafting
    and review skills in Word and the Legal for Cowork plugin (generally
    available 2026-10-15). They show what the platform can do for legal
    work; they are a starting point.
  - The four properties, as a bulleted list with bold labels, each with its
    own fact and source:
    - **Embedded and connected.** Copilot works where legal work is drafted
      and reviewed, and draws on mail, files, meetings, and chats.
    - **In the organization's tenant and under its control.** Identity,
      permissions, and data stay inside the tenant.
    - **Governed like the rest of the organization's data.** The same
      compliance, audit, and protection controls apply.
    - **A choice of models.** Claude models and OpenAI's GPT models today,
      and the models to come, including the frontier models Microsoft has
      announced for enterprise work.
  - One sentence on capacity: legal AI built on the organization's
    Microsoft agreement is provisioned and managed by the organization,
    which matters to users who run out of capacity on individual
    subscriptions.
  - The structural contrast, unnamed: a separate legal AI application runs
    in the vendor's environment, under the vendor's governance, on the
    vendor's choice of model.
- **Exhibit:** none.

### Section 2: Legal AI on Microsoft is built in layers, from enterprise governance to legal skills

*The context for the build: the Microsoft components, the legal platform's
modules, and where legal AI sits on them. The reader should finish this
section able to draw the stack.*

- **Claim:** legal AI that a legal team can rely on is built in layers:
  Microsoft's enterprise services and governance at the base, a legal
  platform of systems of record and a decision support ontology in the
  middle, and legal skills, tools, and agents on top. Copilot and Cowork
  reach across all of them.
- **Evidence and structure (Exhibit 1 carries it; the text unpacks it in
  the exhibit's order, bottom to top, under bold labels that repeat the
  exhibit's layer names):**
  - **Enterprise governance.** Microsoft Entra ID for identity and access,
    conditional access, Purview for data protection, retention, and audit,
    and the tenant boundary. Everything above inherits it.
  - **Microsoft 365 and Power Platform.** Outlook, Teams, Word and the
    Office apps, and SharePoint, where legal work already happens; Power
    Apps and Dataverse, on which business applications are built inside the
    same tenant.
  - **The legal platform: systems of record.** Matter and case management;
    document management; finance (budgets, invoices and e-billing, outside
    counsel spend). Each is a system of record for its part of legal work,
    whether Spaarke provides it or the organization already runs it.
  - **The legal platform: the decision support ontology.** The model of
    what legal operations works on (matters, documents, invoices,
    obligations, policies) and the relationships among them, bound to the
    systems of record where the data sits, with a record of the decisions
    taken on them.
  - **Legal AI.** Skills, playbooks, agents, and tools, running in Copilot
    and Cowork, drawing on every layer below.
  - The challenge (the concession), stated as a challenge: legal teams are
    asked to show AI results faster than they can build this stack, and
    assembling it for legal work takes legal and technical expertise few
    legal teams have on staff. The accelerator starts at the top layer,
    on Microsoft's governance and services, and the platform layers follow
    (section 5).
- **Exhibit:** Exhibit 1, titled as a finding, for example "Legal AI on
  Microsoft is built in layers, from enterprise governance to legal
  skills." A stacked diagram, bottom to top: enterprise governance;
  Microsoft 365 and Power Platform; the legal platform (systems of record
  and the decision support ontology); legal AI. Copilot and Cowork shown
  reaching across the layers. The accelerator's footprint highlighted on
  the top layer. No Microsoft logos; names only.
- **Cross-link:** In our earlier article, Why We Built on Microsoft, we...
  (moves here from section 1 if it fits better); In our earlier article,
  [The Legal Operations Intelligence Ontology](/why-spaarke/legal-operations-ontology),
  we... for the decision support ontology.
- **Voice notes:** describe the systems of record as capabilities, without
  presenting "two modes" or a migration path; Spaarke provides system of
  record capabilities and connects to the systems an organization already
  runs where they serve well. No internal entity, service, or module code
  names.
- **Word budget:** 650.

### Section 3: The Spaarke Legal AI Accelerator puts the platform to work in days

*The required section on the accelerator, its structure, and its
components.*

- **Claim:** the accelerator is a rapid deployment of pre-built legal
  skills, tools, and connectors, with forward-deployed legal engineers, in
  three layers.
- **Evidence and structure (H3 subsections):**
  - Opening paragraph: what the accelerator is; one sentence that it is
    part of the Spaarke Legal AI Accelerator solutions, also available for
    Claude and ChatGPT.
  - **H3: Three layers of legal content.**
    - The foundation: core legal skills every practice uses (review against
      a playbook, build a playbook, issues list, key-terms extraction,
      document comparison, chronology, matter brief, citation and
      verification), with governance and evaluation. Written and maintained
      by Spaarke.
    - Practice packs: specialty skills with expert tuning by Spaarke
      practitioners for Commercial Agreements, Transactions, Real Estate,
      Litigation, and Intellectual Property, and an invoice review pack for
      legal operations. Written and maintained by Spaarke.
    - The team's own playbooks: its positions, fallbacks, escalation rules,
      and house style, captured in a guided setup interview, kept in files
      the team owns, and never overwritten when Spaarke updates the other
      two layers.
  - **H3: What's included.** The bulleted list of technical components
    (bold labels; the second of at most three such lists): legal skills
    (Agent Skills format, deployed as plugins to Cowork and Copilot Chat);
    playbooks for Word; knowledge library (SharePoint, in the tenant);
    Copilot agents per practice; calculation tools and MCP connectors;
    setup interview; evaluation set; governance and deployment package;
    adoption materials.
  - **H3: Tools for the work that must be exact.** The tool claim: skills
    tell the AI how to do the work; deadlines, rent schedules, and invoice
    arithmetic must come out the same on every run, so tools compute them.
- **Exhibit:** none (the components list does the work).

  we explained why...
- **Word budget:** 1,000.

### Section 4: Legal teams can deploy the accelerator themselves or with forward-deployed legal engineers

- **Claim:** the accelerator fits how the team wants to start, and it can
  start a legal AI program or extend one under way.
- **Evidence:**
  - Self-deployed: an attorney adds a practice pack in Cowork and shares it
    with colleagues; a practice team adds the knowledge library and agent.
  - With forward-deployed legal engineers (defined in plain words): a
    one-week engagement that runs the Spaarke Method once, for one practice,
    ending with results tested on the team's own documents against a timed
    baseline; and admin deployment for organizations that install
    centrally.
  - Start or extend: the accelerator builds on skills, prompts, and
    documents the team already uses.
  - Recommendation (stance rule 8): which path suits which team.
- **Exhibit:** none.
- **Cross-link:** the Method article (if not used in the opening).
- **Word budget:** 450.

### Section 5: Everything the accelerator builds carries forward onto the Legal Operations Intelligence platform

- **Claim:** the accelerator is the first step on the maturity path,
  because what it builds is the team's own and connects to the systems of
  record and the decision support ontology as the platform layers are put
  in place.
- **Evidence:**
  - Our earlier article said more tools at Level 2 keep a department at
    Level 2. The accelerator does not add a tool; it builds the team's
    positions, knowledge, and evaluations on the platform it already runs.
    The legal platform layers in Exhibit 1 are where Levels 3 to 5 are
    built.
  - Explicit, system by system, what each artifact connects to:
    - **Matter and case management.** Skills that today ask the lawyer
      which matter a document belongs to read the matter record instead:
      parties, budget, deadlines, assigned counsel.
    - **Document management.** The knowledge library and the documents a
      skill reviews sit in the document system of record, with its
      versions, permissions, and retention.
    - **Finance, budgeting, and e-billing.** Invoice review moves from a
      file reviewed in a session to invoices posted against the matter and
      its budget, with spend visible across firms.
    - **The decision support ontology.** The team's playbooks become
      versioned policies that are cited on each decision; obligations and
      critical dates become obligations with owners; issues lists and
      approvals become entries in a decision record.
  - Exhibit 2 walks five things the work produces across three stages.
- **Exhibit:** Exhibit 2, titled "Everything the accelerator builds carries
  forward onto the Legal Operations Intelligence platform". Columns:
  Copilot for the individual; the team's legal AI with the accelerator; the
  legal platform (systems of record and the decision support ontology).
  Rows: the playbook; critical dates and obligations; the issues list;
  invoice review; matter context. Each cell in the third column names the
  system of record or ontology element it lands in.
- **Cross-link:** In our earlier article, From Reactive to Predictive, we
  set out the five levels...
- **Voice notes:** describe the connection as the design of the
  accelerator and the platform; no claim that the binding is live,
  copy-free, or automatic.
