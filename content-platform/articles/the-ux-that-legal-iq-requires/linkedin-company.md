Conversational AI is one part of the user experience. Treating it as the whole user experience is what makes most "AI-native" demos fall flat.

The vendor opens an unchanged application — a matter list, an invoice screen, a contract repository — and adds a chat panel on the side. That is a UI change. It is not a UX change. The workspaces underneath are unchanged.

For attorney drafting and document Q&A, conversation matches the work. That was the right place for the first wave of generative AI to land. But AI in an enterprise system of record now does much more.

Summaries on the matter card. Similar-matter suggestions at intake. Anomaly flags on invoices. Routed tasks across workflows. Insights inside dashboards. Most of the AI value in a system of record does not live in a chat thread — and the workspaces where it does live have not been redesigned.

Two kinds of AI matter for the user experience. Generative AI produces content. Agentic AI takes action. Each requires a different interaction treatment. Neither is well-served by a chat panel alone.

Legal Operations Intelligence requires a different shape: three coordinated surfaces — Assistant, Workspace, and Context — plus AI inside the content itself. Users engage with all of it from inside the work.
