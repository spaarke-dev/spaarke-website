## Spine

The argument in three to five sentences. The first sentence is the
thesis, and it should reappear almost unchanged in the opening.

> We argue that the foundation of legal operations intelligence is a shared model of the entities a department works on, the relationships among them, and the actions it may take on them. That model is the one layer that people, business intelligence, and AI agents can all read from and act through. Most departments have a matter table, and few hold the relationships among their records or the record of what the department decided about them, who acted, and under which policy; business intelligence reports what happened and does that job well, and the model adds the decision record that business intelligence then reads as well. AI has lowered the cost of building the model and agents have raised the value of having one, because an agent that reads governed entities has less room to invent them and every agent shares one vocabulary. An AI model can be replaced through configuration, whereas the semantic work that makes a department's data intelligible takes years and belongs to the department.

### Section 4 (candidate heading): The unit of value is a governed decision
- **Claim:** Business intelligence computes the variance correctly in both runs of the example, and the entity and action model adds the place to act and the record of what was decided, which across 200 matters and four quarters becomes evidence the department could not otherwise assemble.
- **Evidence:** The worked example, run twice, from the sentence "This matter is running over budget; ask outside counsel to investigate." In the first run business intelligence computes the variance and shows the matter is over budget, and there is nowhere to act and no record of what was done: someone opens the report, switches to Outlook, writes to the firm from memory, gets a reply in two days, and notes it in a spreadsheet, and next quarter the system knows nothing more than it did. In the second run the entity and action model is underneath: the same variance is a fact on the matter, computed deterministically and carrying its provenance; a policy evaluates it into an observation linked to the matter, the invoice lines, and the engagement; a proposed budget inquiry action goes out through its gate; the reply is classified back onto the inquiry thread; and a typed outcome (write-off, accrual revision, approved scope change, or no action) is recorded against the matter. Across 200 matters and four quarters that outcome history is the outside counsel report card, which exists only because each intervention was captured as an entity, and it returns to business intelligence as new input.
