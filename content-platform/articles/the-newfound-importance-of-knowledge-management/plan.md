> We argue that a legal department's own knowledge has become the context on which the accuracy of its AI systems depends, and that curating that knowledge for AI is now part of the legal operations mandate. Legal operations already carries the responsibility in most departments, and it already runs the systems in which the new sources of knowledge are created. A model becomes specific to a department through grounding, instruction, example, and evaluation, each of which consumes a knowledge asset that someone has to maintain, and retrieval over curated content still leaves roughly one answer in five wrong, so the quality of the corpus and the ability to test outputs are management responsibilities. What counts as knowledge has widened past finished work product to material the department already produces and never wrote as knowledge, and a document that grounds an answer needs a known author, date, status, and permissions, because the system applies it without the judgment a lawyer would have applied. Curation also decides what AI must not see, and privilege, ethical walls, stale content, and retention each become a rule about what enters the corpus, who may retrieve it, and when it is retired.

### Section 7: A document that grounds an answer needs an author, a date, a status, and permissions
- **Claim:** A document kept for human reuse needed to be findable and roughly right, because the lawyer who reused it applied judgment; a document that grounds an AI answer needs a known author and date, a known status (current, superseded, or withdrawn), known permissions, and where possible a known outcome, because the system applies it without that judgment.
- **Evidence:** The technical root: retrieval pipelines cut documents into passages small enough to embed and search, starting from "512 tokens (approximately 2,000 characters)" with 25% overlap (Microsoft Learn, Azure AI Search chunking page, ms.date 2026-06-08, updated 2026-08-31; confirmed, raw text). A passage loses its document-level wrapper of author, date, status, and permissions unless the pipeline carries that metadata with every chunk; that inference is the article's own and is presented as discussion. Josh Baxter of NetDocuments described the result of uncontrolled copying as "micro and incomplete DMSs" (CCBJ, 2026-08-31; confirmed), which Calve glossed as "partial representations of institutional knowledge assembled for particular applications and potentially governed differently," and that is the failure mode a curation standard exists to prevent. Two worked examples, one paragraph each, as illustrations of process: the superseded clause (an indemnity position withdrawn after a regulatory change stays in the repository; a lawyer would recognize it as out of date, a retrieval system with no status field retrieves and applies it; the example shows why status is a curation criterion) and the intake answer (a business unit asks whether a vendor contract needs a data processing addendum, legal answers by email, another unit asks six months later; where the earlier answer was kept with its author, date, and status, the assistant grounds on it and cites it, and the department can show which source produced the answer, which is what provenance means in practice). Keep the plane count at nine or fewer and the threads to one per plane, because a convergence motif tends toward a starburst.
