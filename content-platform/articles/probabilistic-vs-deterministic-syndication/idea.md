## What this is

A LinkedIn syndication that promotes the 2026-05-21 blog post **Legal AI Is Not
Deterministic — And That Matters**. Same argument, compressed to a LinkedIn-
native post (~200–400 words), with a CTA back to the article.

## Why now

Per the 2026-05 launch campaign distribution sequence, blog posts get a
syndication post ~3 business days after publish (cf. the 5/22 syndication of
the 5/19 piece, the 5/29 syndication of the 5/26 piece). 5/25 (Monday) is the
right slot — clean gap from the 5/22 syndication, doesn't crowd the 5/26 blog.

## Hook candidates

Strong opening lines pulled from the source article. The brief stage picks one.

1. *"Deterministic software works like a calculator. Probabilistic AI works
   more like an experienced advisor."* — the parallel-framing move; carries
   the title's argument in two sentences.

2. *"A billing rule engine that rejects invoices over a rate cap is
   deterministic. An AI system that identifies 'potentially non-compliant
   billing patterns' is probabilistic. Both are useful. Both can be accurate.
   But they are fundamentally different from each other."* — the legal-domain
   pair; lands the relevance immediately.

3. *"Generative AI is probabilistic. That isn't a defect. It's the property
   that determines where AI belongs in legal work — and where it doesn't."*
   — the take, compressed; works as a one-line hook with the body underneath.

4. *"Legal AI does not fail because it is probabilistic. It fails when the
   people using it do not know that it is."* — the close of the source
   article; works as an inversion hook if we want the post to read as a
   stand-alone provocation rather than a "read the new article" promotion.

My pick (subject to brief-stage review): **#3** as the hook — declarative,
sets up the "And That Matters" payoff. Optional: open with #1 as a
quote-style lift-up, then pivot.

## Body beats (compressed argument)

The LinkedIn post is a compressed version of the source article — 200–400
words, not a re-write. Beats to cover:

- The distinction in one move (calculator vs experienced advisor, or the
  billing example).
- Where each belongs in legal work — one-line gestures, not the full
  ten-bullet treatment from the article.
- The agentic implication — one sentence on how a probabilistic step inside
  an agent compounds.
- The "make it visible" payoff — one sentence on what good legal AI tools
  should surface.
- CTA back to the full article.

## What this should NOT become

- A copy-paste of the article's opening into LinkedIn. The post is a
  compression, not a substitution.
- A full ten-bullet re-listing of deterministic vs probabilistic candidates.
  Pick one or two examples — the article's job is exhaustiveness.
- A "schedule a demo" close. The CTA is the article URL.
- A founder-voice post under the org byline. If we want founder voice, that's
  a separate parallel post from `ralph-schroeder`.
