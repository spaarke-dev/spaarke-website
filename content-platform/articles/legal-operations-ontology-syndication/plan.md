## Spine

A department is deciding about two layers at once. The AI model it runs
turns over within the year and costs little to swap. What the department
means by "matter", "budget" and "obligation" took years to settle, is
specific to this company, and in the article's words cannot be bought from
a vendor. Departments run a long procurement on the first layer and almost
none on the second, so each tool ends up defining "matter" by default and
nobody chose that. The post names the asymmetry and sends the reader to the
article for the argument.

## Format

Article syndication, 200 to 250 words, per `content-types/linkedin-post.md`
section 2. The teaser is its own hook and does not summarise the piece. The
link sits in the post body on its own line, never in a comment.

## Hook

Fixed by the brief and used verbatim. It is a two-line contrast, which the
brief designates as the one permitted instance of that shape in this post.

> Your AI model will be replaced within the year. Your definition of
> "matter" will not.
>
> One of those is the department's intellectual capital, and most
> departments are letting each tool set it by default.

## Body beats

One to three sentences per beat, one blank line between, per
`content-types/linkedin-post.md` section 5.

### Beat 1: the asymmetry, stated as cost
- **Claim:** The layer everyone is shopping for is the layer that changes
  most often and costs least to change.
- **Carries it:** The contrast in replacement cost, not a statistic. The
  brief bans figures here so the evidence stays in the article.
- **Watch:** State it positively. Do not build it as "it is not the model,
  it is the definitions", which is rule 14.

### Beat 2: why the definitions are the department's own capital
- **Claim:** The definitions take years to accumulate and no vendor can
  supply them, because they encode how this department actually works.
- **Carries it:** The naming of the three objects the article closes on:
  matter, budget, obligation.
- **Cross-link:** none; this is the article's territory.

### Beat 3: who decides
- **Claim:** The department and the general counsel own these definitions.
  Legal operations is what makes the decision happen rather than what makes
  the decision.
- **Source of the posture:** `voice/stance.md` section 2, the authority
  boundary. Facilitates, catalyzes, builds, manages. Never "owns" or
  "decides" on the department's behalf.
- **Watch:** This is the beat most likely to slide into a Spaarke pitch.
  The article names no Spaarke capability by design, and neither does this.

### Beat 4: the series frame
- **Claim:** This is article three of five, and the five together are a
  developed position rather than one article with a thesis.
- **Carries it:** Naming the series, not listing the other four titles.
  The other four are referenced but not individually linked, per the
  brief's References section.

## Close

Per `content-types/linkedin-post.md` section 6, a syndication closes on the
read prompt with the link on its own line. No open question, because the
goal here is the click rather than the comment thread.

- **Close beat:** The decision the reader is actually facing, followed by
  the link. Ends on the reader's own next move, not on a Spaarke claim and
  not on a pair of clipped symmetrical sentences.
- **Link:** https://www.spaarke.com/why-spaarke/legal-operations-ontology
- **Hashtags:** `#LegalOps #LegalTech`, on their own line after a break, as
  metadata rather than as the close.
