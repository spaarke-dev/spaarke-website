# Topic

A LinkedIn carousel that introduces Spaarke on the company page: what the
platform is, how it is built and deployed, and how an engagement runs. It is
adapted from the Spaarke Overview Slides decks, consolidated and redrawn for a
phone screen.

# Angle / Point of view

A legal department's most valuable asset is the record of how it decides, and
today that record is scattered across systems or never kept. Spaarke builds a
model of those decisions, one decision at a time, on Microsoft, and the
department owns the model. Technology, consulting,
and managed services are three ways into the same model, which is the claim
that separates Spaarke from a software vendor and from a consulting firm.

The refrain, repeated almost verbatim at the cover, the glance slide, and the
close: **one model of your department's decisions.** The plan fixes the
wording and the draft holds it.

The carousel is one argument told through its parts, which keeps it from
reading as a product tour. Every body slide answers the question the slide
before it raises: what is wrong, what Spaarke is, what the model is, where the
data comes from, what it looks like on a real decision, who decides, where it
runs, and how the work is done.

# Why now

The Spaarke Method overview published on 2026-10-05, and its campaign has no
company-page asset that introduces Spaarke as a whole. The overview decks were
revised on 2026-10-21 and now carry the glance slide, the invoice review use
case, and the five Method phases, so the material is current. A carousel puts
that material in front of LinkedIn readers who will not open a 14-slide deck.

# Must include

- The cover as the hook and the thumbnail. It names Spaarke, legal operations,
  and Microsoft, so a reader scrolling past knows what it is.
- Technology, consulting, and managed services joined by one ontology that
  the department owns.
- One architecture: Spaarke data and third-party systems of record, plus
  external legal information, feed the ontology. Decisions, BI, and automation
  draw on it.
- Invoice review as the single worked example, because it shows a guideline
  becoming a rule and a rule becoming a recorded decision. Intake by PDF or
  LEDES upload and by e-billing integration may be shown: the writer confirmed
  on 2026-10-21 that work in progress may appear.
- AI-directed, human-controlled, in those words or close to them
  (`vocabulary.md`, section 1): AI proposes, and a person confirms.
- Deployment as two options: in the customer's own Microsoft tenant, or
  hosted by Spaarke.
- The five Spaarke Method phases by name, and "forward-deployed legal
  engineers" once, defined in plain words.
- A close that asks the reader which decision they would model first, with the
  article link in the post text.
- Post text of about 150 to 250 words above the document: the hook, two or
  three sentences of setup, the link, and the hashtags on their own line.

# Must NOT include

- More than 10 slides. Consolidate rather than cut the argument: the full
  deck's market, segments, and requirements slides fold into the problem
  slide, and partners and services fold into the glance and Method slides.
- Screenshots of the website diagrams, or any text smaller than about 40px on
  the 1080-wide canvas. Diagrams are redrawn as large, simple shapes.
- More than about 40 words on a body slide.
- "Two deployment modes", a migration path, or any wording that sets the
  Spaarke system of record against third-party integration as alternatives.
- The outside firm whose operating model Spaarke's services follow, its
  product names, or "bootcamp".
- "E-billing platform" for Spaarke. Invoice review is a use case of the
  ontology.
- Named customers, invented figures, or an implied engagement.
- Sales close, "schedule a demo", emoji, exclamation marks, more than three
  hashtags.
- Em dashes, en dashes, and the constructions in `voice/examples/ai-tells.md`.
  Slide copy is short, which makes tagline contrasts (`X, not Y`), fragment
  stacks, and colon reveals the main risk. Every body slide states its idea in
  complete sentences.

# Voice notes

Company byline, so the post text uses the organizational "we" for Spaarke's
own positions, and the slides address the reader as "you" where they describe
the reader's department. The register is plain and declarative, the house
register compressed. The slides are not fragments.

The claim that a department's decision evidence is scattered and its reasoning
rarely kept is held from practice. It carries no citation, which
`style-guide.md`, section 5, rule 12 permits because the problem slide states
the mechanism with it (separate systems, nothing joining them).

# Hero graphic

The carousel is built as designed slides, not generated images. It uses the
overview decks' brand: dark #0a0a0a and light #f6f6f4 grounds, the Spaarke blue
#4060DC with #82A5EB, Inter Tight for headings, Inter for body text, and
JetBrains Mono for eyebrows. The cover and close are dark, the body slides
alternate light and dark only where a slide's content calls for it, and each
slide carries a small page marker and the Spaarke logo.
