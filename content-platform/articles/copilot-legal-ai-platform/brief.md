# Topic

How Microsoft's AI platform is put together for legal work and why it is a
strong foundation for a legal team's AI. The article explains the parts, how
they work together on a real piece of legal work, and what a legal team adds
to make the platform its own.

It is one of three companion pieces on building legal AI on Microsoft, with
`implementing-copilot-for-legal` and `copilot-compared-legal-ai-platforms`.
The pieces are linked by subject and theme. None of them numbers itself or
refers to "the series".

# Angle / Point of view

The premise the three pieces share, and that later pieces on Anthropic,
OpenAI, and Google will share: an AI platform gives a legal team more
flexibility and capability to build, manage, govern, and use its AI than a
legal AI application does. The frontier providers now supply both the general
platform and core legal components on top of it, and for many legal teams that
is the better approach.

The argument of this piece is that Microsoft's platform is a strong choice for
legal work, for four reasons:

1. The documents, email, meetings, and chats that make up legal work are
   already stored and worked on in Microsoft's applications.
2. Work IQ, Microsoft's enterprise intelligence layer, gives Copilot access to
   that content within each user's existing permissions and the organization's
   governance.
3. Copilot appears consistently across the applications people use, so the
   same skills and playbooks work in Word, Outlook, Teams, and the Copilot app.
4. Business users can build with low-code tools and developers with
   professional tools, on the same platform and under the same governance.

The piece is objective in that every claim is sourced and every limit is
stated, and it takes a clear position: these four strengths are real, and the
article explains why each matters to a legal team.

**The phrase to repeat** in the opening, in the heading of the section that
makes the main case, and in the close: "the work, the content, and the
controls are already in place" (wording for the writer to settle).

# What has changed

2026 has been the year the frontier providers introduced legal solutions on
their platforms. Microsoft made its legal tools available through the Frontier
early-access program in early 2026, and Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google each
introduced legal offerings during the year. The providers will keep adding
legal solutions and building blocks designed to be configured, extended, and
customized, which is what makes the choice of platform matter more than the
choice of any single tool. The opening states this as the change and moves
straight to the argument. It does not anchor on any single release date.

# The direction legal teams are taking

This section replaces the template's "concession" (writer ruling 2026-11-12:
present it as the direction legal teams are moving, not as a fault).

Legal teams are moving to capture their knowledge and intellectual capital in
a form AI can use: the positions they hold and the ones they will trade, their
precedents, their playbooks, and the way their experienced lawyers review a
document. A platform makes that knowledge reusable across every place the team
works. Capturing it well takes people who understand both the legal work and
the platform, which is the role of forward-deployed legal engineers and legal
operations experts. The piece introduces that role here, with one link to our
earlier article on the Spaarke Method, and describes what they do: write the
team's positions into skills and playbooks, curate the precedents, connect the
team's systems, and set up the evaluations that show whether the AI is getting
the work right.

- **The work already lives in Microsoft's applications** (strength 1). Word,

- **Work IQ gives AI governed access to the organization's content**
  (strength 2). Introduce Microsoft's enterprise intelligence concept in one
  or two sentences and use the name Work IQ. Do not list Fabric IQ, Foundry IQ,
  or Web IQ. Copilot sees what each user is already permitted to see, and
  sensitivity labels, DLP, audit, retention, and eDiscovery apply to AI
  interactions as they apply to the rest of the content. Make governed
  collaboration and governed AI the point: the same rules govern who works on
  a document with whom and what AI can do with it. Agent 365 extends the same
  oversight to agents.
- **Copilot works the same way across the applications** (strength 3). One
  Copilot app on web, desktop, and mobile, and Copilot inside each
  application. The ways of working, from a quick question in Chat to
  multi-step work in Cowork to an always-on agent in Autopilot, and Microsoft's
  advice to use the least setup that does the job. Consistency is the point: a
  playbook written once works in a Word review, a Cowork batch review of 40
  agreements, and an intake automation.
- **The legal layer.** The legal skills and partner plugins Microsoft ships for
  Word and Cowork, grouped by what they do (drafting, review and negotiation,
  obligations, research into guidance, moving matters forward). The redlining
  engine uses a deterministic resolution layer rather than having the model
  generate every edit. Use it to state both of our positions on AI
  (`stance.md`, rule 9): that AI is changing how legal work is delivered, and
  that legal work needs precision today's models do not guarantee, which is why
  a fixed, repeatable method for applying edits matters. Link
  [Legal AI Is Not Deterministic](/why-spaarke/probabilistic-vs-deterministic)
  in the first person.
- **Both business users and developers can build** (strength 4). From skills a
  lawyer writes in plain language, to Agent Builder, to Copilot Studio, to Code
  and the Copilot Managed Runtime, to Microsoft Foundry for developers. Plugins
  package skills, agents, connectors, and MCP servers, and IT approves them in
  one registry. The organization chooses among OpenAI and Anthropic models.
  Detail on choosing a tool belongs to `implementing-copilot-for-legal`.
- **Capturing the team's knowledge**, with forward-deployed legal engineers and
  legal operations experts, as set out under "The direction legal teams are
  taking". Link the knowledge management article and the Spaarke Method article
  in the first person.
- **How the parts work together** (Exhibit 2). Consider an in-house contract
  request: a sales lead emails legal; an automation starts a Cowork task that
  checks the request against the playbook; Copilot pulls the counterparty's
  draft from Outlook; Word produces tracked-change redlines with comments; the
  cover email waits for a lawyer's approval; the new version is filed in
  SharePoint or the DMS; the obligations tracker is updated; and the whole
  exchange is recorded for audit. Present tense, opened with "Consider", no
  invented figures.
