# Topic

Ralph Schroeder's personal takeaways from The LegalTech Fund Summit 2026 (Scottsdale, November 16 to 18), written as a founder and a legal technology provider who attended more than a dozen sessions.

# Angle / Point of view

A balanced snapshot, chosen by the writer on 2026-11-19. The post opens on the one impression that ties the event together (change is under way in both the practice and the business of law, whether a reader calls it a threat or an opportunity) and then gives a short list of takeaways of roughly equal weight. The takeaways are observations from the room, held from practice, and they are not ranked. The post credits the event plainly and keeps the writer's own judgments in the first person.

# Why now

The Summit closed on 2026-11-18. A takeaways post is worth most in the week after the event, while attendees are still comparing notes and the people who did not attend are reading the coverage.

# What the post asserts from practice

Every takeaway is the writer's own observation from the event, and none is attributed to a session or a person. The post states them flat, in the first person, with the reason where one is needed (`style-guide.md`, section 5, rule 12):

- **The event.** The content was excellent: more than a dozen sessions, each substantive and well prepared. For a legal technology provider it is a strong forum to connect, learn, and keep up with the market, although it does not replace time with customers.
- **The audience.** The Summit leaned toward law firms more than corporate legal departments, although many of the products on show serve both.
- **The scale.** Attendance of more than 1,000 people **TBD — confirm** and hundreds of legal technology providers, from startups to growth-stage companies, show a field with real depth.
- **The theme.** TLTF's Pathways initiative framed the program with its film "The Future of the Business of Law 2040" **TBD — confirm** (exact title and format), which the writer found thought-provoking.
- **The change.** Whether people call it an existential threat or a generational opportunity, there is a palpable sense that the practice of law and the business of law are both changing.
- **AI-native delivery.** AI-native products remain the main focus, and that now covers technology-enabled legal services as well as software and tools.
- **ABS and MSO.** Alternative business structures, and management services organizations in particular, were the hot topic. The writer attended almost every such session, and each was standing room only.
- **The frontier model companies.** Hearing the frontier model companies describe where their vertical legal offerings are heading should lead many teams to reconsider how their technology stack is architected, deployed, managed, and used. Spaarke is doing exactly that. The writer did not see Microsoft or Google on the program (writer decision, 2026-11-19: keep as written).
- **The investment market.** Investment in legal remains historically active but is maturing, and it is less frenetic than in 2025 and early 2026, in step with the wider market for AI-driven business software. Many people said deals are taking much longer. A number of late-stage private equity holdings should come to market, but many were bought at peak valuations, and the gap between bid and ask is forcing sellers to adjust expectations.
- **The billable hour.** Its long-predicted end came up in session after session. Clients and new AI-native law firms are pressing for better answers, and the question is tied to the MSO discussion. The post reports that pressure and does not take a side (writer decision, 2026-11-19; `domain-knowledge.md`, section 7).
