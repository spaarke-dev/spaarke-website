## Ralph's notes, as written

I'm back from the TLTF Summit 2026. I want to write a 'my key take aways from the Summit' for a LinkedIn blog post; here are my main points

* there was so much to take in and these are only a snapshot; these are my thoughts and reactions and not attributed to any particular session or person

* first, this was a fantastic event; the content was excellent; i attended at least a dozen sessions and everyone was both substantively useful and well prepared and presented; no duds
* for a legal solutions provider an excellent forum to connect, discuss, learn, and stay up on what is happening in the market; not as a replacement for meeting customers but very worthwhile
* the conference seemed to be more law firm oriented versus corporate legal, although of course many solutions support both sides of the house
* if judged by the number of attendees (upwards of 1,000), the legal technology space is robust; 100s of legal tech solution providers from startups to growth stage
* the summit's theme was encapsulated in the very well produced 'The Future of the Business of Law 2040' by TLTF's Pathways initiative; very thought provoking
* whether you call it an existential 'threat' or generational 'opportunity', there is a palpable feeling that there is real change afoot in both the practice of law and the business of law
* AI-native solutions continue to be the primary focus; importantly this is for both software/tools and technology enabled services
* the 'hot topic' was ABS and particularly MSO; i sat in on almost every session and each was standing room only
* it was interesting and very insightful to see the AI frontier model companies; hearing their views on where their vertical solutions are heading should have many consider or re-consider how the tech stack is architected, deployed, managed and used (I know we are!); I didn't see Microsoft or Google
* investment activity in legal remains historically very active, although maturing and less frenetic or frantic as compared to 2025 and early-2026; tracking the overall investment conditions seen in the AI driven, broader business solution market; many expressed that deals are taking significantly longer; there are a number of late-stage PE investments that should be coming to market; but many of these were bought at the valuation peak and given today's cooled down market, the bid/ask gap is forcing adjusted expectations
* the forever 'death of the billable hour' was a consistent topic of discussion; it does seem as if better answers must come soon because clients (companies) and new law AI native law firms are demanding or pushing an answer; it may not be a tomorrow but the shift must be imminent; this is not disconnected from the MSO discussion

## Public context from the Summit agenda

The program ran tracks on AI economics, private equity in law, ABS and
MSO structures and their regulation, the deal market, build versus buy
for legal AI, knowledge management, and access to justice, with fireside
chats from frontier model providers. The post draws only on Ralph's own
reactions above; the agenda is context for the drafter, and no session
or speaker is attributed.
