> Managing legal operations effectively now means directing the changes under way in the legal department, in the legal function's place in the business, and in the outside counsel relationship. Each of those changes reaches legal operations as a decision about how legal work gets done, and every such decision depends on information the department can act on. Six drivers push the three changes in the same direction, with a new, AI-driven view of what the department needs in people and roles as the largest of them, and the published frameworks remain the profession's shared vocabulary while practice has moved past their texts. Effective management therefore means sizing the legal judgment the business needs at the points where it decides, deciding what the business may do on its own so that legal stops being the bottleneck wherever technology manages the risk and the business proceeds informed and with legal as its partner, running the department so that the general counsel's time goes to the business, choosing deliberately what goes to outside providers, and curating the knowledge that people and AI systems depend on. ## Opening

The opening gives a situation that the reader accepts, then the
complication, and then the answer, which is the thesis. The thesis
arrives within the first 250 words of a long-form article and within
the first 150 words of a short post, and in either format no later
than the third paragraph. Sketch each paragraph as a beat of one or
two sentences. A short post may combine the complication and the
frame in one paragraph.

The shape matches the situation, complication, answer model in
`consulting-register.md` section 6.1, which happens to be on this
subject. The shape may match and the sentences may not, so the draft
reuses none of that passage's wording, and it anchors the situation
on a sourced sentence rather than on a recital of the frameworks.

- **¶1, hook:** The situation the reader accepts: legal operations has a recognized scope and the reader runs it. Anchor it on a sourced observation from the brief rather than on the frameworks. The candidate is the Thomson Reuters Institute's 2025 Legal Department Operations Index (2025-09-24; 128 US responses), which reports 82% of departments with at least one dedicated legal operations role and says, in a confirmed sentence that may be quoted exact, that "Legal operations work is expanding beyond its origins as primarily a cost-control function to include a focus on systems, processes, and technology." The frameworks the reader knows (CLOC and ACC) are named in a clause, not walked. If the quotation is used here it is not repeated in Section 1.
- **¶2, complication:** Three things around the function are changing at once, and the reader meets them together: the legal department (more demand on flat resources, AI in daily work, a new view of what it needs in people and roles), the legal function's place in the business (legal, and risk in particular, treated as a critical consideration of the business; a general counsel who contributes to strategy, direction, and decision making; a business that takes a direct part in its own legal risk), and the outside counsel relationship (what companies send out and what they expect back). Harbor's finding that AI is in place at 98% of departments while the operating model has not kept up is the frame the piece takes: each change is an operating-model decision, and each arrives at legal operations. Underneath the three changes, the role and contribution of the general counsel and the legal department to the business are changing, and so is what the business expects from legal in how legal issues and requirements are addressed, with technology driving the shift toward integrated handling and self-service for certain legal activities that legal historically handled directly (the brief's Topic; writer's direction, 2026-09-22). That underlying change may take one clause here if the opening has room, and it is developed in Sections 3 and 4 either way.
