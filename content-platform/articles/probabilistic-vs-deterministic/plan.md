## Spine

> Generative AI doesn't calculate — it predicts. The same prompt can produce different outputs because the model is sampling from learned patterns, not running a rulebook. That property determines where AI belongs in legal work, where deterministic systems still own the answer, and how the two have to be combined. It also explains why agent systems compound uncertainty across tool calls — and why the most reliable legal AI patterns are hybrid, with deterministic guardrails at the points that have to be enforceable. Well-designed legal AI makes the probabilistic layer visible so practitioners can act responsibly on what it produces.

### Section 1 — What deterministic and probabilistic actually mean

- **Claim:** The two terms aren't synonyms for "reliable" and "unreliable." They name two different kinds of computation, both useful, suited to different jobs. Generative AI is the probabilistic kind.
- **Evidence:** The locked opening pair — **calculator** (deterministic: same inputs, same answer, every time, on every device) and **experienced advisor** (probabilistic: predicts the most likely or most appropriate response from patterns across enormous amounts of information, but not with mathematical certainty). Then the locked legal pair — a billing rule engine that rejects invoices over a threshold (deterministic) vs an AI system that identifies "potentially non-compliant billing patterns" (probabilistic). Close the section with the reframe: probabilistic systems can be extraordinarily useful and often highly accurate — but they are fundamentally different from systems designed to produce guaranteed outputs.

### Section 2 — Where each belongs in legal work

- **Claim:** Match the kind of computation to the job. Deterministic systems own the answer wherever the result has to be the same every time and wherever a rule has to be enforceable. Probabilistic systems earn their place wherever judgement, surfacing, drafting, and pattern-recognition outperform brittle rules.
- **Evidence:** Two parallel lists, each using the library's bolded-lead-in pattern (`style-guide.md` §4):
  - **Deterministic candidates** — matter intake routing rules; conflicts checks; OCG compliance enforcement (billable-rate caps, block-billing rejection); invoice math and accruals; deadline calculations; access control, ethical walls, matter-level permissions.
  - **Probabilistic candidates** — first-pass drafting; matter history summarization; related-matter and precedent surfacing; classification and tagging; drafting recommendations for human review.
- **Cross-link:** [What Attorneys Need to Know About AI](/blog/2026-02-15-what-attorneys-need-to-know-about-ai) — the prerequisite-companion piece on the four architectural decisions a legal team makes whether it knows it or not.

### Section 3 — The most useful legal AI patterns are hybrid

- **Claim:** The strongest patterns combine both — probabilistic surfacing paired with deterministic enforcement, on the same record. The probabilistic step adds reach; the deterministic step makes the decision auditable.
- **Evidence:** Walk one concrete example end-to-end. Billing is the natural one (it's already the locked example pair from §1, so the reader has the cognitive frame): an AI system surfaces a likely OCG violation in an incoming invoice — line items that *look like* block-billing or rate-cap violations across thousands of invoices. A deterministic rule engine then applies the cap, flags the line, and routes it for human review. Each half does what only it can do. The AI couldn't enforce; the rule engine couldn't surface. Land the architectural point: this is why the [Legal IQ Stack](/why-spaarke/the-iq-stack) separates Data (mostly deterministic), Memory (mixed), and Inference (probabilistic) — the separation is the architectural answer to where the deterministic gate sits.

### Section 4 — What this means for agents

- **Claim:** A probabilistic step inside an agent loop doesn't stay one step — small uncertainties compound across tool calls and decisions. An agent is a chain of probabilistic choices unless deterministic guardrails or human gates are placed deliberately at the consequential points.
- **Evidence:** Picture an agent doing matter intake routing — half a dozen probabilistic decisions stacked (this matter type, that team, this conflict signal, that priority, this counsel assignment), each with some uncertainty, each compounding into the next. The single-step error rate looks tolerable; the end-to-end error rate doesn't. Then the operational implications: bound the agent's scope, pin critical tool calls to deterministic systems (the conflicts check is not a guess; the deadline calculation is not a guess), require an explicit human gate before any action that writes to the operational record. Avoid quantitative claims about agent error rates — flag any percentage claim that surfaces in drafting as `**TBD — confirm**` until a citable source is found.
- **Cross-link:** [The AI Readiness Gap](/blog/2026-03-01-the-ai-readiness-gap) — the structural-readiness companion; agents compound the readiness problem and make the data architecture more, not less, load-bearing. Optional second link: [AI Without Giving Away the Keys](/blog/2026-02-22-ai-without-giving-away-the-keys) if word budget allows.

### Section 5 — What good AI tools should make visible

- **Claim:** Practitioners can only act responsibly on AI output if the probabilistic layer is visible to them. Good AI tools surface that layer rather than hiding it under a confident UI.
- **Evidence:** Five concrete UI/UX patterns, written as the library's bolded-lead-in pattern (`style-guide.md` §4):
  - **Grounded citations** — show the source the answer rests on, not just the answer.
  - **Inference markers** — make clear where the system is inferring vs where it's reading from the record.
  - **Reproducibility logging** — capture the prompt + context + model so the same answer can be re-produced even when the output is non-deterministic.
  - **Consequential-gate human review** — require a human checkpoint at the points where the output writes to the operational record, not just at final approval.
  - **System-of-record fact vs inference** — distinguish them visually and architecturally so the user knows which is which.
- **Cross-link:** None required in-section — the §4 cross-link carries enough weight. Optional: link [AI Without Giving Away the Keys](/blog/2026-02-22-ai-without-giving-away-the-keys) at the close.
- **Voice notes:** Tighter than §4 — this is the practical-implication coda. Each bolded lead-in is one short clarifying sentence after it; resist piling up examples.
- **Word budget:** ~250 words.
