# Author calibration: the Legal Operations Intelligence series

The writer reviewed and rewrote all five articles of the Legal Operations
Intelligence series in September 2026. This file holds the two tables he
produced from that pass. They are calibration, not theory: every "before" is a
sentence a pipeline draft actually contained, and every "after" is what he
replaced it with.

Read this file when drafting or reviewing a long-form article. If a sentence you
are about to write resembles a "before", it will be rejected.

The five articles themselves are the exemplars for voice, structure and length.
`examples/house-exemplar.md` annotates article 1 trait by trait; this file is
the sentence-level companion to it.

Where this file and a rule elsewhere in `voice/` disagree, this file is the more
recent judgment. Raise the conflict rather than resolving it silently.

## 1. AI-speak: patterns to eliminate

These patterns were removed from every article. Treat them as defects. When one is found, **cut it or say the point plainly. Do not polish it into a better aphorism.**

| Pattern | Example removed | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Aphoristic closing line | "A firm that has never been asked has nothing to answer." | Cut, or state the practical point plainly: "Departments that want to see AI savings will need to ask for them." |
| "Not X but Y" / "not X. It is Y" | "The answer is not to remove legal from the decision. It is to give…" | State Y directly: "The better answer is to agree in advance which activities…" |
| Colon reveal | "…but the intent is plain: hold the size…" | "…but they show a clear plan to hold the size…" |
| "X is not new. What is new is Y" | "None of this is new in kind. What is new is the response available." | "Budget pressure is not new, but the options for responding to it are." |
| Cleft sentence | "What these sources add is the reasoning…" | "These sources add the reasoning…" |
| Tidy parallel pair | "Routine work shows whether the operating model runs. Unplanned events show whether it is managed." | "An operating model also has to hold up when something unplanned happens." |
| "Matters less than" / "only as useful as" | "Which surface carries the output matters less than whether…" | "Whatever the surface, the data point behind it has to mean the same thing." |
| Cliché framings | "AI has changed both sides of that equation." / "The stakes rise with automation." | State the specific change: "AI has lowered the cost of building the model and increased the value of having one." |
| Dramatic short sentence | "Adoption is moving quickly." / "Only the department can supply that." / "Stored as it is, it does little." | Cut, or fold the point into the preceding sentence. |
| Restating summary | "A business leader asked a commercial question, legal answered… and the leader decided." | Cut; the example already showed it. |
| Filler | "It is harder than it sounds." / "The two do different jobs." | Cut. |
| Section-intro clichés | "Four gaps illustrate the distance." / "The work is being staffed before it has been named." | Write a specific intro that states the point. |
| "We hear" | "Among the most notable trends we hear from general counsel…" | "One of the most notable trends in corporate legal departments is…" |
| Finale framing | "This final article…" | "As part of our series on building the legal operations intelligence capability…" |
| Clever wit as closer | "…which is equally available to the other side of a negotiation." | Plain version: "…which gives the department no advantage." |

**Allowed exceptions:** a contrast or crisp line may stay when it carries the section's core argument and is concrete, for example "a paralegal may need to read every invoice on a matter and approve none of them". The author kept a small number of these deliberately. When in doubt, flag the line as *optional* in the voice audit rather than deciding alone.

## 2. Before and after

| Before (draft) | After (final) |
|---|---|
| Legal operations was built to control cost. | The mandate of legal operations is evolving rapidly. |
| Three things around the function are changing. | Three pressures are converging on the function. |
| The general counsel needs evidence the C-suite can see | Visibility and insight strengthen the partnership between legal and the business |
| The gap still matters, because the executives who do not see the contribution approve the department's budget. | If the business does not recognize the value the law department provides, it is less likely to support the department's budget and resource needs. |
| Few legal departments have scaled AI beyond pilots or can measure its value. | Legal departments are actively deploying AI, both generative and agentic, and significant investment is driving the imperative to demonstrate return. |
| Business intelligence is the part of legal operations that most departments already practice. | Business intelligence is easy to take for granted. |
| Business intelligence is well established in spend analytics, but service… measures are reported by fewer than one in five departments. | Because budgets and outside counsel spend are always a top priority, spend analytics tends to predominate. Service, cycle time, risk, and business outcomes are equally important focus areas. |
| Dedicated tooling has spread less widely than the practice. | As the need for broader business intelligence grows, departments are expanding their footprint and capabilities. |
| The readers of legal's numbers now include… | The board, the executive team, finance, IT, and the business units all rely on legal's information… |
| A reporting environment supplies the first ingredient well and leaves the other three to whoever reads the report. | A reporting environment delivers the data but leaves unaddressed the reasoning, action, and policy control that management actually requires. |
| Relationships make the model readable. Acting through it requires something more. | Relationships make a legal operating model intelligible. An operational platform also has to provide a governed way for people and AI agents to act through them. |
| A dashboard that shows a matter is over budget is doing its job, and a department without one is worse off. | While dashboards are useful, they cannot provide the more sophisticated insights the ontology supports, nor can they directly support decisions that span multiple systems. |
| No single system can supply that context, and the reason is structural. | No single system can provide the required context. This limitation is inherent in the architecture of legal systems. |
| Context reaches the platform in two ways, and a platform needs both. | Building context into the platform requires two paths. |
| To be effective, AI requires knowledge that is accurate, concise, and comprehensive. | To be effective in legal work, AI needs knowledge that is specific to the company… not general public sources. |
| Legal departments have managed knowledge for years, mostly for training, procedures, and the reuse of precedent. | Legal departments have a well-established knowledge management capability focused on collaboration, sharing, and codifying best practices and internal standards. |
| The first step is smaller than any platform strategy: choosing which decision to model first. | The practical first step is to choose which decision to model first. |
| An ontology that sits above… existing systems is only as useful as its connections to them. | An ontology that sits above a department's existing systems depends on its connections to them. |
