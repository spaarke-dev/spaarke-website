# The Spaarke Method

## Theme

The Legal Operations Intelligence series argued what a law department should build and why. This series describes how Spaarke does the work with a customer, in specific terms. The first article sets out the method: five phases (envision, design, build, deploy, and manage) that form a loop, one decision at a time, with experienced practitioners and the organization's own end users taking part from the beginning, legal engineers working alongside them, and technology, consulting, and managed services joined through one ontology that the customer owns. Each later article takes one phase in detail. The series applies equally to law departments and to law firms, and it treats the AI capability an organization builds for itself as a product of the same phases.

A reader who follows the series should come away believing that a decision support platform is a different kind of solution from a system of record, that it is built with the people who make the decisions, and that the method for building it can be described precisely enough for a buyer to judge.

## Why now

The conversation about AI in legal has moved from adoption to operating discipline on both sides of the engagement, and a buyer evaluating any provider needs to know how the work is organized as well as what is built. The five earlier articles answer the second question, and the October 20 field report (`state-of-legal-operations-fall-2026`) describes where the field stands. This series answers the first.

## Narrative arc

1. **The Spaarke Method: Envision, Design, Build, Deploy, and Manage** (overview). The structure, the people, and the capability the method builds.
2. **Envision** (working title). How a department or a firm chooses its first decision and finds the decisions nobody owns.
3. **Design.** Agreeing definitions, the hard part of an ontology, and settling what a matter is.
4. **Build.** A first working model on real data, and the questions that test it.
5. **Deploy.** Putting AI into proposing mode, and how autonomy widens.
6. **Manage.** Running the platform, changing definitions, and reading the decision record.

Further articles are open and not yet defined. Candidates are legal engineers and the practitioners they work with, building the internal AI capability, working with outside counsel and how a firm builds its own platform, and what changes when the data comes from the Spaarke system of record and when it comes from third-party systems. A worked engagement follows once a customer can be referenced.
