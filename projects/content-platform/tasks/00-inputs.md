# 2. Audience personas (3–4)

## Persona: General Counsel / Deputy General Counsel (Mid-Market to Enterprise)

- **Role:** Senior legal executive responsible for risk management, legal department performance, outside counsel oversight, and operational modernization. Usually pragmatic, politically aware, and highly risk-sensitive.
- **What they’re hired for:** Reducing operational friction, improving visibility, managing outside counsel spend, supporting business growth, and maintaining governance/compliance.
- **Skepticism:** AI hype, unrealistic automation claims, vendor jargon, “replace lawyers” narratives, and products that create more operational complexity.
- **Reads:** ACC publications, HBR, Bloomberg Law, Thomson Reuters Institute, LinkedIn, McKinsey Quarterly.
- **Vocabulary they use:** operational visibility, governance, outside counsel management, legal spend, matter management, risk, business alignment.
- **Vocabulary they dislike:** disruption, transformation, AI magic, replace lawyers, 10x productivity.

---

## Persona: Director / Head of Legal Operations

- **Role:** Responsible for legal systems, workflow modernization, reporting, billing compliance, vendor management, intake, and operational rigor across the legal function.
- **What they’re hired for:** Making legal measurable, operationally efficient, scalable, and visible to the business.
- **Skepticism:** AI vendors who don’t understand real legal workflows, products requiring large implementation projects, disconnected tools, and platforms that don’t integrate with Microsoft.
- **Reads:** CLOC, ACC Legal Ops, LinkedIn legal ops communities, Law.com, Artificial Lawyer, Thomson Reuters Legal Business.
- **Vocabulary they use:** workflow orchestration, intake, e-billing, OCG compliance, automation, dashboards, spend analytics, matter lifecycle.
- **Vocabulary they dislike:** paradigm shift, synergies, unlock value, AI-powered everything.

---

## Persona: CIO / Legal Technology Director

- **Role:** Technology leader responsible for legal systems architecture, security, governance, integration, and enterprise technology alignment.
- **What they’re hired for:** Reducing technology sprawl, improving governance, ensuring integration with Microsoft infrastructure, and managing risk.
- **Skepticism:** SaaS silos, shadow AI tools, weak security models, disconnected AI systems, and products not aligned with Microsoft enterprise architecture.
- **Reads:** Microsoft architecture guidance, Gartner, CIO.com, enterprise architecture communities, Azure and Power Platform technical content.
- **Vocabulary they use:** governance, architecture, identity, orchestration, extensibility, Microsoft-native, tenant isolation, operational platform.
- **Vocabulary they dislike:** black box AI, low-code magic, autonomous everything.

---

## Persona: Managing Partner / Law Firm Operations Leader

- **Role:** Responsible for law firm operational performance, client service delivery, profitability, staffing efficiency, and collaboration with clients.
- **What they’re hired for:** Improving operational efficiency while preserving high-quality client work and realization.
- **Skepticism:** Corporate legal departments using AI only as a fee-reduction mechanism; operational tools that damage attorney-client collaboration.
- **Reads:** Law.com, American Lawyer, LegalTech News, BTI Consulting, LinkedIn.
- **Vocabulary they use:** realization, staffing leverage, client collaboration, billing compliance, service delivery, matter execution.
- **Vocabulary they dislike:** commoditize lawyers, replace firms, eliminate legal labor.

# 4. Do-not-claim / do-not-say list

- “Replace lawyers” — fundamentally inconsistent with our human-in-the-loop philosophy and alienating to the legal market.

- “10x productivity” — unverifiable and overly simplistic. Our story is operational intelligence and improved execution.

- “AI-powered” — too generic by 2026. We should explain specifically what the AI is doing operationally.

- “Transform your legal department” — empty consulting language. Show the operational improvements instead.

- “Autonomous legal AI” — overstates the current maturity of AI systems and undermines trust.

- “The future of law is fully automated” — we believe the future is AI-directed and human-controlled.

- “One-click legal work” — unrealistic and unserious for enterprise legal operations.

- “Disrupting legal” — sounds like a startup outsider perspective rather than a credible operational platform.

- “ChatGPT for legal” — dramatically undersells the platform and incorrectly positions Spaarke as primarily conversational AI.

- “Copilot for lawyers” — too narrow and increasingly commoditized positioning.
