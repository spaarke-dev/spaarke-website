# Spaarke content platform — research sources

> Repository of authoritative surveys, benchmarks, and reports we cite
> in long-form content. Updated as new editions release. Each entry
> records the canonical claim we'd cite, the exact source, and the
> date checked.

Last updated: 2026-05-07.

## How to use

- When a brief calls for a stat, look here first.
- If a stat in this file is older than 18 months, treat as stale —
  re-verify before citing.
- New report? Add it here when you read it.
- Verify each stat by reading the source page. Don't cite figures
  carried by intermediary blogs or vendor compilations.

## M365 Copilot adoption — broad enterprise

### Source: Microsoft FY25 Q1 earnings call (October 30, 2024)
- **What it says**: 70% of the Fortune 500 had adopted Microsoft 365
  Copilot at the time of the call. Microsoft's framing: "adoption
  rates exceed those of previous Microsoft 365 suite launches."
- **URL**: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2025/earnings-fy-2025-q1
- **Date of source**: 2024-10-30
- **Date checked**: 2026-05-07
- **How we cite**: "More than 70% of the Fortune 500 had adopted M365
  Copilot by late 2024 (Microsoft FY25 Q1 earnings call)."
- **Caveat**: Microsoft-defined "adoption" = license purchase, not
  enterprise-wide deployment. Most Fortune 500 deployments are pilots
  or phased rollouts, not org-wide rollouts. Cite alongside the
  Lighthouse "Beyond the 70%" framing when accuracy matters.

### Source: Microsoft 2026 Work Trend Index annual report (May 5, 2026)
- **What it says**: 420 million monthly active Copilot users in Q1
  2026, up from 230 million in Q1 2025. 15 million paid M365 Copilot
  seats. 15× year-over-year increase in active agents on Microsoft
  365; 18× in large enterprises.
- **URL**: https://news.microsoft.com/annual-work-trend-index-2026/
- **Date of source**: 2026-05-05
- **Date checked**: 2026-05-07
- **How we cite**: "Microsoft 365 Copilot crossed 15M paid seats and
  420M monthly active users in early 2026 (Microsoft Work Trend Index
  2026)."
- **Caveat**: Microsoft is the publisher; "monthly active users" is
  Microsoft-defined and includes free-tier consumer Copilot. Paid
  enterprise seat number (15M) is the more conservative figure.

### Source: Microsoft 2025 Work Trend Index — "Year of the Frontier Firm" (April 23, 2025)
- **What it says**: 24% of leaders say their companies have already
  deployed AI organization-wide; 12% in pilot. 82% of leaders view
  the year as pivotal for rethinking strategy and operations. 81%
  expect moderate-to-extensive agent integration within 12-18 months.
  Survey: 31,000 knowledge workers across 31 countries.
- **URL**: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born
- **Date of source**: 2025-04-23
- **Date checked**: 2026-05-07
- **How we cite**: "By early 2025, only about a quarter of leaders
  reported organization-wide AI deployment, with most enterprises
  still in pilot mode (Microsoft Work Trend Index 2025)."
- **Caveat**: Microsoft-published; respondents are knowledge workers
  broadly, not Fortune 500 specifically. Self-reported.

## M365 Copilot adoption — corporate legal specifically

- **What it says**: Among AI-adopting in-house legal departments,
  Microsoft Copilot is used by 40%, second to ChatGPT at 74%. 38% of
  surveyed corporate legal teams already use AI tools; 50% are
  actively exploring. 256 in-house legal professionals surveyed
  (~87% Canada, ~10% US).
- **URL**: https://www.lawnext.com/2025/06/legal-departments-show-growing-ai-adoption-but-implementation-challenges-remain-new-survey-finds.html
- **Date of source**: 2025-06-05
- **Date checked**: 2026-05-07
- **How we cite**: "Among in-house legal departments using AI, ~40%
  use Microsoft Copilot — second only to ChatGPT (Counselwell/Spellbook
  2025 Benchmarking Report)."
- **Caveat**: Heavy Canadian skew (87%); small sample (n=256); vendor
  co-published (Spellbook is a legal-AI vendor). Use as directional,
  not authoritative for US Fortune 500.

### Source: Wolters Kluwer Future Ready Lawyer 2026 (March 10, 2026)
- **What it says**: 92% of legal-professional respondents use at
  least one AI tool daily; 62% report time savings of 6-20% of work

### Source: ACC × Everlaw, "In-House Counsel Use of GenAI" (October 29, 2025)

## Outside-counsel spend / leakage

### Source: ACC / Major, Lindsey & Africa Law Department Management Benchmarking Report (2024 edition, June 2024)
- **What it says**: Median total legal spend rose from $3.1M to
  $3.8M year-over-year. Median total legal spend at companies with
  >$20B revenue rose from $50.8M to $80M (a 57% increase). Average
  internal/external split: 53% inside, 47% outside; large companies
  (>$20B revenue) flip closer to 56% outside. n=449 legal departments
  across 24 industries, 20 countries.
- **URL**: https://www.legaldive.com/news/legal-spend-benchmarking-outside-counsel-association-of-corporate-counsel-major-lindsey-and-africa/653260/
- **Date of source**: 2024-06 (published)
- **Date checked**: 2026-05-07
- **How we cite**: "Companies with >$20B in revenue saw legal spend
  jump from $50.8M to $80M (ACC/MLA 2024 Law Department Benchmarking
  Report)."
- **Caveat**: ACC member panel (skews to larger departments and
  better-organized legal-ops functions). Self-reported.

### Source: 2026 ACC Chief Legal Officers Survey (January 2026)
- **What it says**: 45% of CLOs plan to increase outside-counsel
  spend in 2025 — a 17-point jump from 28% the prior year, reversing
  years of in-sourcing cost-containment. n=1,049 CLOs across 43
  countries. The 2026 edition also reports 63% of CLOs expect
  headcount to remain stable, leaning on AI and upskilling instead.
- **URL**: https://www.acc.com/resource-library/2026-acc-chief-legal-officers-survey
- **Date of source**: 2026-01-29
- **Date checked**: 2026-05-07
- **How we cite**: "After years of bringing work in-house, 45% of
  CLOs now plan to increase outside-counsel spend (2026 ACC CLO
  Survey)."
- **Caveat**: ACC member panel; member self-selection bias.

### Source: Thomson Reuters 2026 Report on the State of the US Legal Market (January 2026)
- **What it says**: US law-firm demand grew at the fastest rate since
  the Global Financial Crisis; worked rates rose 7.3%; average firm
  profit grew 13%. Technology spend rose ~10% YoY; talent costs rose
  8.2%. Profits per lawyer at Am Law 100 firms rose 53.7% since 2019.
- **URL**: https://legaltechnology.com/2026/01/07/thomson-reuters-state-of-the-us-legal-market-report-record-profits-and-increasingly-unstable-ground/
- **Date of source**: 2026-01-07
- **Date checked**: 2026-05-07
- **How we cite**: "Worked rates at US law firms rose 7.3% in 2025,
  the fastest since the financial crisis (Thomson Reuters 2026
  State of the US Legal Market)."
- **Caveat**: Thomson Reuters publishes the report and sells products
  used in the underlying data. Cite carefully; don't conflate
  rate-growth with corporate spending growth (different metrics).

## Corporate-legal data difficulty / unstructured-data prevalence

### Source: 2025 EY Law General Counsel Study (April 2025)
- **What it says**: 52% of legal departments report disorganized
  data; 44% report disconnected legal-business platforms; 41% report
  lacking access to accurate data. 75% are actively developing or
  refining legal technology and data strategies. n=1,000 GCs/CLOs at
  $1B+ revenue companies across 21 countries; fielded Nov 2024 -
  March 2025.
- **URL**: https://www.ey.com/en_gl/newsroom/2025/04/ey-law-study-reveals-disruptors-prompting-the-evolution-of-legal-departments-and-the-key-barriers-to-change
- **Date of source**: 2025-04-09
- **Date checked**: 2026-05-07
- **How we cite**: "More than half of legal departments — 52% —
  report disorganized data; 75% are actively rebuilding their tech
  and data strategies (2025 EY Law General Counsel Study)."
- **Caveat**: EY Law is a vendor in the legal-services market —
  treat the survey as legitimate research but acknowledge author
  interest. Strong methodology and sample (large companies, n=1,000).

### Source (legacy / archival): EY Law Survey 2021 with Harvard Law School Center on the Legal Profession
- **What it says**: 94% of legal departments said they did not have
  the data and technology needed to optimize their department.
- **URL**: https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/expert-insights/making-the-future-brighter-for-your-legal-department
  (intermediary; the 94% figure traces to the 2021 EY/Harvard study,
  not to a Wolters Kluwer original survey)
- **Date of source**: 2021
- **Date checked**: 2026-05-07
- **How we cite**: Avoid in current pieces — figure is ~5 years old.
  Use the 2025 EY Law GC Study figures instead (52% disorganized
  data, 75% reworking data strategy).
- **Caveat**: The "94%" figure has been widely re-quoted (often
  attributed loosely to "Wolters Kluwer") but the primary source is a
  2021 EY/Harvard study. Even when correctly attributed, it is now
  too dated to defend without re-verification.

## In-house legal headcount growth (decade trend)

### Source: ACC, "US In-house Counsel Population Statistics" (September 30, 2025)
- **What it says**: US in-house counsel population grew from 78,000
  in 2008 to 145,000 in 2024 — an 87% increase. Comparable growth in
  the same period: law firms +23%, government legal +38%. Source data
  is the US Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Employment and
  Wage Statistics (OEWS) annual report.
- **URL**: https://www.acc.com/about/newsroom/news/association-corporate-counsel-in-house-population-report
- **Date of source**: 2025-09-30
- **Date checked**: 2026-05-07
- **How we cite**: "US in-house counsel population nearly doubled
  between 2008 and 2024, growing 87% — far outpacing law-firm (23%)
  and government (38%) growth (ACC, citing US BLS data, 2025)."
- **Caveat**: BLS OEWS is a robust government data source; ACC's
  analysis is a re-presentation. Period covers 16 years, not a clean
  "decade" — adjust the framing accordingly when citing. The "~30%
  growth over the past decade" phrasing previously used in our domain
  knowledge significantly understates the actual figure.

## In-house attorney turnover

### Source: ACC, "The State of Stress Among In-house Legal Professionals" (December 15, 2025)
- **What it says**: Among in-house lawyers reporting high stress,
  24% indicate they plan to leave within the next year. Only 1 in 5
  in-house counsel report sufficient staffing. Drawn from the 2025
  ACC Law Department Compensation Survey; n=1,600+ US-based in-house
  lawyers and legal-ops professionals.
- **URL**: https://www.acc.com/about/newsroom/news/new-report-examines-stress-landscape-among-house-legal-professionals-impacting
- **Date of source**: 2025-12-15
- **Date checked**: 2026-05-07
- **How we cite**: "Among high-stress in-house counsel — a majority
  of the population — 24% plan to leave within a year (2025 ACC State
  of Stress Among In-house Legal Professionals)."
- **Caveat**: Intent-to-leave is a leading indicator, not realized
  turnover — actual departure rates run lower. Use as a turnover-risk
  signal, not as a "20% of in-house attorneys turn over each year"
  factual claim. The previous "20%" figure in our domain knowledge
  conflated several looser measures.

### Source: Axiom, "A Wave of Attrition Expected to Hit In-House Legal Teams" (November 2024)
- **What it says**: 28% of in-house lawyers in teams without ALSP
  support are actively job-hunting; 14% in teams using ALSPs are.
  Roughly half of in-house legal professionals report they are either
  actively or passively seeking new roles.
- **URL**: https://www.axiomlaw.com/resources/press-releases/research-a-wave-of-attrition-expected-to-hit-in-house-legal-teams
- **Date of source**: 2024-11-12
- **Date checked**: 2026-05-07
- **How we cite**: Sparingly. Axiom is a legal-services vendor
  whose product offering (ALSP support) is the protagonist of the
  research framing. Useful only as directional context.
- **Caveat**: Vendor-published; the framing favors Axiom's product
  category. Cite numbers, not narrative.

  across professional-services respondents expect the same. Top use
  cases: document review (77%), legal research (74%), document
  summarization (74%). Only 41% have a gen-AI usage policy; only 40%
  provide gen-AI training. n=1,702 across professional-services
  sectors; legal = 41% of respondents. Field period: Jan-Feb 2025.
- **URL**: https://www.lawnext.com/2025/04/thomson-reuters-survey-over-95-of-legal-professionals-expect-gen-ai-to-become-central-to-workflow-within-five-years.html
- **Date of source**: 2025-04
- **Date checked**: 2026-05-07

  doubled in 2025 — 14% to 26% — while 78% expect it to become
  central to their work within five years (Thomson Reuters Institute,
  2025 Generative AI in Professional Services)."
- **Caveat**: Thomson Reuters is a vendor with legal-AI products;

## Sources we evaluated and rejected

For transparency. Listed so we don't accidentally re-introduce them.

- **The "94%" Wolters Kluwer data-difficulty figure.** Trace-able
  only to a 2021 EY Law / Harvard Law School Center on the Legal
  Profession survey. Re-quoted in Wolters Kluwer commentary (which
  led to the misattribution). Now too old (~5 years) to defend.
  Replaced by 2025 EY Law GC Study figures (52% disorganized data,
  75% rebuilding tech/data strategy).

- **"20% in-house attorney turnover annually."** No primary source
  validated this figure as a clean annual-turnover rate. The closest
  defensible figures are intent-to-leave indicators (24% high-stress
  in-house lawyers planning to depart, ACC 2025) and active
  job-hunting figures (~28% in unsupported teams, Axiom 2024). The
  underlying institutional-knowledge-loss point still holds; the
  "20%" number does not.

- **Vendor "X% productivity gain" claims (5x, 10x).** Per
  domain-knowledge §6, we don't cite these. Reaffirmed.

- **Stackmatix, AI Business Weekly, Electroiq stat compilations.**
  Aggregator sites that re-quote primary sources; useful for
  discovery, not for citation. Always trace through to the original.
