## Project

Spaarke is a legal operations intelligence platform for legal departments and their outside counsel. It runs on Microsoft 365 (Dataverse, SharePoint, Azure AI Foundry) and consolidates matters, documents, spend, performance assessment, and AI into one shared system. Currently in early access.

## Strategic position

**The moat:** Spaarke is the only legal platform built for both sides of the engagement. Every other vendor sees one side — the firm's billing, or the department's spend, or the firm's documents, or the department's matter list. Spaarke sees both, which is what enables cross-client benchmarks and shared intelligence no single-sided tool can produce.

**The category claim:** *The shared system of record for legal.* Not "for" Microsoft 365 — *on* it. Spaarke is foundational. AI tools (Microsoft 365 Copilot, Harvey, Legora, custom agents) run on top of it.

**What we don't claim:** Not a Copilot accessory, not a contract review tool, not an e-billing platform, not "AI for legal." Those are crowded categories owned by other vendors.

## Brand vocabulary

These phrases should appear consistently across the entire site:

- *Both sides of every matter* — recurring tagline (hero, callbacks, closing)
- *The shared system of record* — category claim
- *Legal departments and their outside counsel* — long-form audience naming
- *In-house and outside counsel* — short-form audience naming
- *Their outside counsel / its outside counsel* — possessive forms
- *Fluent in legal* — AI capability claim
- *On Microsoft 365* — preposition matters; Spaarke is on the substrate, not for it

## Visual direction

**Aesthetic:** Sophisticated enterprise, *Bloomberg Businessweek* and *Monocle* references, not SaaS marketing. Dark mode is preferred — Spaarke's product UI is dark and the brand should feel cohesive.

**Typography:** Sans-serif, two weights only (regular and medium). Sentence case throughout — never Title Case, never ALL CAPS. Slight tight letterspacing on headlines (around -0.02em).

**Color:** Restrained. Single accent color permitted. No gradients, no glow effects, no neon, no noise textures, no decorative gradients behind text.

**Spacing:** Generous. Whitespace is the primary visual element.

**Visual rhythm:** The page should alternate dense and breathing sections. Hero (breathing) → The gap (dense, stat-heavy) → What Spaarke is (medium) → Platform (dense, modular) → AI (medium-heavy) → Both sides (medium) → Insights (medium) → Closing (breathing).

## Things to avoid

- AI-led headlines (*"AI for legal," "The AI workspace for lawyers"*) — Harvey, Legora, Hebbia own that ground
- Microsoft-led headlines (*"Legal-grade Microsoft 365"*) — Harvey took this in March 2026 with their Copilot integration
- Plugin / extension / add-on language anywhere — Spaarke is foundational, not subordinate
- Three-verb taglines (*"Work Smarter. Operate Leaner. Decide Faster."*) — generic SaaS
- Stock photography aesthetic — diverse-team-around-laptop, handshakes, pointing-at-screen
- AI-generated people in imagery — real brand-damage risk for a legal-AI company
- Title Case in headlines — sentence case throughout
- *"The firms they hire"* or any phrasing that frames outside counsel as vendors — use *outside counsel* or *their outside counsel*
- *"Engagement vs invoice"* framing — too clever, doesn't resonate

**Primary CTA:** Get access *(high contrast, solid)*

**Layout:**

- Two CTAs centered below the subhead
- Full-bleed Spaarke product screenshot below the text block
- Trust strip (one quiet line) directly below the screenshot
- Reference patterns: Linear, Stripe, Vercel, Notion — confident, restrained, dark-friendly

### Section 2 — The gap

**Section heading:** The gap. *(Or:* What's actually happening in legal operations*.)*

**Intro line:** Demand is rising. Resources aren't. The release valves are gone.

**Stats (large format, horizontal layout):**
- **+63%** demand in regulatory work *(CLOC 2026)*
- **+58%** demand in cybersecurity *(CLOC 2026)*
- **32%** of departments expect attorney headcount growth *(CLOC 2026)*
- **1 hour/day** lost switching between tools *(LegalLeaders 2025)*

**Closing line:** Legal operations is the lever. Spaarke is the platform.

### Section 3 — What Spaarke is

**Section heading:** One platform. Both sides. Every matter.

**Three pillars (three columns):**

**The matter as the unit of work.** Every document, email, invoice, deadline, and decision lives connected to a matter. Both sides see the same record, scoped by ethical walls and matter-level permissions.

**AI fluent in legal.** Microsoft 365 Copilot — and any frontier model in Azure AI Foundry — given the legal context they need to do real legal work. *(Detailed in Section 5.)*

**Shared by both sides.** The same matter record, the same documents, the same spend signals, the same outcomes — for the legal department and its outside counsel. Ethical walls and matter-level permissions enforced throughout.

**Visual treatment:** Three columns, each with a small diagrammatic icon or product detail at the top, the pillar headline in medium-weight type, and three lines of supporting text. Don't over-illustrate.

### Section 4 — The platform

**Section heading:** The platform.

**Four module cards (2x2 grid), each with a product screen excerpt and one-line claim:**

**Workspace.** *The daily briefing for every matter you own — what's overdue, what's new, what needs you.*

**Finance Intelligence.** *Invoices in. Benchmarks out. AI-extracted line items, OCG compliance, spend signals across firms.*

**Performance Assessment.** *Score every matter on what mattered — outcome, budget, OCG compliance, practitioner contribution.*

**Knowledge & Search.** *Find the document. Find the pattern. Semantic search across every matter, document, and outcome.*

### Section 5 — AI for legal, finally fluent

**Section heading:** AI for legal, finally fluent.

**Subhead:** 40% of in-house legal teams already use Microsoft 365 Copilot. None of them are getting what they need from it alone — because Copilot doesn't know what a matter is.

*Source citation: Counselwell + Spellbook, AI in Legal Departments 2025 Benchmarking Report*

**Four numbered subsections:**

**01. The legal context Copilot doesn't have.** Spaarke gives Copilot what it lacks — matters, parties, OCGs, privilege boundaries, billing structures, outcome data. The same context a senior associate brings to the question. Copilot becomes a lawyer's tool, not a knowledge worker's chatbot.

**02. Choose the model for the matter.** Backed by Azure AI Foundry. Run on OpenAI, Anthropic, or any frontier model in the catalog. Pick the right model for the work — and switch as the AI landscape evolves, without re-architecting.

**03. Inside the perimeter your IT team built.** Built on Dataverse, SharePoint, and Microsoft 365 Copilot — the substrate every legal department already trusts. No new identity, no new permissions, no parallel security model. Spaarke fits inside the governance your IT team already approved.

**04. Bring whatever AI comes next.** Copilot, Harvey, Legora, your own agents — Spaarke makes any AI legal-aware. The platform is durable; the AI choices stay yours as the market shifts.

**Visual treatment:** This section runs longer than others — four substantial subsections rather than three brief ones — because it does the most procurement-objection-handling work. Compensate with clean, generous whitespace between subsections.

## Buyer context

The 2026 legal operations buyer is navigating a *structural productivity gap* (CLOC 2026 State of the Industry Report) — demand rising sharply in regulatory and cybersecurity work while headcount and outside counsel spend are flat. Tool fragmentation is the most visceral pain — 70% of in-house lawyers spend over an hour a day switching between systems (LegalLeaders 2025). AI is now table stakes — 92% of legal professionals use AI daily (Wolters Kluwer 2026 Future Ready Lawyer Report); 40% of in-house teams use Microsoft Copilot specifically (Counselwell + Spellbook 2025). The legal-department-and-outside-counsel relationship is being explicitly redefined around shared data and partnership rather than transactional billing (Wolters Kluwer 2026 trends report). Spaarke is positioned to resolve all four of these forces at once.

This brief covers the **home page only**. Subsequent briefs should extend this template for:
- **Platform page** — the deeper product walkthrough, with each module getting its own expansion
- **Why Spaarke page** — the strategic argument with research, data, and the system-of-record case at depth
