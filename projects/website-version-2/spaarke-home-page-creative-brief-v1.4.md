- Hero subhead, Section 3 cards, and Section 6 closing updated to reflect the *all sides* framing throughout

## Project

Spaarke is a legal operations intelligence platform for the business, in-house counsel, and outside counsel. It runs on Microsoft 365 (Dataverse, SharePoint Embedded, Azure AI Foundry, Foundry IQ) and consolidates matters, projects, documents, communications, spend, performance, AI, and agentic workflows into one shared platform. Currently in early access.

## Strategic position

**The moat:** Spaarke is the only legal platform built across the full legal value chain — business clients, in-house counsel, and outside counsel. Every other vendor sees one party in the relationship. Spaarke connects all three, which is what enables shared intelligence and benchmarks no single-sided tool can produce.

**The category claim:** Spaarke defines a new category — *Legal Operations Intelligence* — and positions its specific instance as *Legal IQ*, the operational intelligence layer that runs on top of Microsoft 365 to make Copilot, AI agents, and existing systems fluent in legal work.

**The architectural claim:** Spaarke is the *shared platform* for legal — built on Microsoft 365, not for it. AI tools (M365 Copilot, Harvey, Legora, custom agents) run on top of Spaarke, not the reverse.

**What we don't claim:** Not a Copilot accessory, not a contract review tool, not an e-billing platform, not "AI for legal." Those are crowded categories owned by other vendors.

## Brand vocabulary

These phrases should appear consistently across the entire site:

- *See all sides of every matter* — the canonical tagline (full sentence form, hero + closing CTA only; see canonical-form rule below)
- *Legal Operations Intelligence* — the category Spaarke defines (capitalized as a proper noun)
- *Legal IQ* — Spaarke's instance of the category, the operational intelligence layer (capitalized)
- *The shared platform for legal* — architectural claim
- *Operational intelligence* — what Spaarke provides (not just AI, not just answers)
- *Built on Microsoft* — preposition matters; Spaarke is built on the foundation, not adjacent to it
- *The business, in-house counsel, and outside counsel* — full-triangle audience naming
- *In-house and outside counsel* — short-form audience naming when business client isn't the focus
- *The business and its legal team* — alternative shorthand
- *The business gets answers. The team gets visibility. The firms get clarity.* — recurring rhetorical structure for the three-stakeholder claim

**Canonical tagline rule:** *See all sides of every matter.* appears in this exact form in the hero, the closing CTA, the meta description, deck covers, and any external positioning. Variations and fragments are permitted only when the form is structurally different (e.g., Section 3's triplet treatment of *One platform. All sides. Every matter.* uses the words as section structure, not as the tagline). The tagline is not recombined into alternative arrangements (such as *All sides. Every matter. One platform.*).

**Words now retired from the brand vocabulary:**
- *Both sides of every matter* — replaced by *See all sides of every matter* to include business clients
- *Fluent in legal* — drafting connotation; replaced by *Legal IQ* and *operational intelligence*
- *AI for legal* as a category — Spaarke is *Legal Operations Intelligence*, not AI
- *Substrate* — jargon; use *foundation* or *built on Microsoft* instead
- *System* as a Spaarke descriptor — use *platform* (Spaarke is a platform, not a system)
- *Workspace*, *Document Intelligence*, *Outside Counsel Engagement*, *AI Workflows*, *Performance Intelligence* — superseded by the new capability names below
- *End the spreadsheet war* — juvenile, doesn't fit positioning tone
- *Engagement vs invoice* framing — too clever, doesn't resonate

## Visual direction

**Aesthetic:** Sophisticated enterprise, *Bloomberg Businessweek* and *Monocle* references, not SaaS marketing. Dark mode is preferred — Spaarke's product UI is dark and the brand should feel cohesive. Section 3 uses a light treatment as a deliberate breath-of-air moment.

**Typography:** Sans-serif, two weights only (regular and medium). Sentence case throughout — never Title Case, never ALL CAPS. Slight tight letterspacing on headlines (around -0.02em).

**Color:** Restrained. Single accent color permitted (used sparingly — Section 5 diagram for Spaarke container and Copilot connection, otherwise minimal). No gradients or glow effects, with one exception: the soft radial behind the Copilot logo in the Section 5 diagram. No neon, no noise textures.

**Spacing:** Generous. Whitespace is the primary visual element.

**Visual rhythm:** The page should alternate dense and breathing sections. Hero (breathing) → The gap (dense, stat-heavy) → What Spaarke is (light, three pillars) → Platform (medium-dense, zigzag with screenshots) → Legal IQ (medium-dense, diagram-led) → Closing (breathing).

## Things to avoid

- AI-led headlines (*"AI for legal," "The AI workspace for lawyers"*) — Harvey, Legora, Hebbia own that ground; Spaarke is positioned as Legal Operations Intelligence, a different category
- Microsoft-led headlines (*"Legal-grade Microsoft 365"*) — Harvey took this in March 2026 with their Copilot integration
- Plugin / extension / add-on language anywhere — Spaarke is foundational, not subordinate
- Three-verb taglines (*"Work Smarter. Operate Leaner. Decide Faster."*) — generic SaaS
- Stock photography aesthetic — diverse-team-around-laptop, handshakes, pointing-at-screen
- AI-generated people in imagery — real brand-damage risk for a legal-AI company
- Title Case in headlines — sentence case throughout
- Phrasing that frames outside counsel as vendors (*"the firms they hire"*)
- Excluding the business client from audience descriptions
- Eyebrow labels that reference page structure (*"All five run on..."*) — every section should make sense in isolation
- Per-card CTA buttons in Section 3 (the strategic synthesis section) — they interrupt synthesis; let Sections 4 and beyond handle deeper engagement

## The home page

The home page is six sections plus footer. Each section does one thing well.

### Section 1 — Hero

**Top banner:** Now accepting early access partners — request access today

**Headline:** See all sides of every matter.

**Subhead:** The shared platform for legal departments and their outside counsel.

*(Note: subhead optimizes for tightness rather than full three-stakeholder naming; Section 2 and Section 3 expand the audience explicitly. Optional longer alternative: "The shared platform for the business, in-house counsel, and outside counsel.")*

**Primary CTA:** Get access *(high contrast, solid)*
**Secondary CTA:** Watch demo *(outline)*

**Trust strip below product:** Built on Microsoft 365 · Copilot-native · Azure AI Foundry — OpenAI, Anthropic, and any frontier model

**Layout:**
- Centered headline and subhead with generous whitespace
- Two CTAs centered below the subhead
- Full-bleed Spaarke product screenshot below the text block
- Trust strip (one quiet line) directly below the screenshot
- Reference patterns: Linear, Stripe, Vercel, Notion — confident, restrained, dark-friendly

### Section 2 — The gap

**Section heading:** Demand is rising. Visibility isn't.

**Intro paragraph:** In-house legal is squeezed from every side — the business expects faster service, AI demands new safeguards, and outside counsel relationships demand transparency none of the legacy tools provide.

**Four stats (2x2 grid, large numerals, source attributions on each card):**

**77%** — of in-house teams face increasing workload and complexity *— while business clients expect faster turnaround on every request.* (Axiom 2026 Global In-House Legal Study)

**1 in 5** — legal departments has reached AI maturity *— even as 75% have raised AI budgets and 66% are accelerating adoption.* (Axiom 2025 Legal AI Report)

**79%** — are pressured to cut outside counsel spend *— but 57% can't quantify the savings they achieve.* (LegalBillReview / In-House Connect, 2025)

**87%** — of external legal budget goes to outside counsel *— with most teams lacking real-time visibility into where it goes.* (ACC / MLA Benchmarking Report)

**Closing line (left-aligned with thin accent rule):** Spaarke closes the gap — across the business, the legal team, and outside counsel.

**Note:** All four percentages should be verified against the source PDFs before going live.

### Section 3 — What Spaarke is

**Section heading:** One platform. All sides. Every matter.

*This heading serves as the umbrella for both Section 3 (the three pillars) and Section 4 (the five capabilities). Section 4 has no standalone heading. Each card below anchors to one phrase of the section title.*

**Three pillar cards (three columns), each with three layers — verb-led headline, operational claim, and a callback line that resolves a Section 2 pain. No per-card CTAs.**

**Card 1 — One platform.**

*Headline:* One platform for the work, the data, and the AI.

*Operational claim:* Matters, projects, documents, spend, and AI — connected on one Microsoft 365-native foundation that serves the business, the legal team, and the firms they work with. Replace four point solutions with one platform that knows what a matter is.

*Callback line:* No more bolt-ons. No more parallel logins. No more *which tool was that in again?*

**Card 2 — All sides.**

*Headline:* All sides of the engagement, finally aligned.

*Operational claim:* Business clients, in-house counsel, and outside counsel — working in shared, secure spaces where the matter, the documents, the tasks, and the outcomes live in one record. Ethical walls and matter-level permissions enforced throughout.

*Callback line:* Partnership replaces reconciliation. The business gets answers. The team gets visibility. The firms get clarity.

**Card 3 — Every matter.**

*Headline:* Every matter, every project, every detail.

*Operational claim:* The full scope of legal work — matters, projects, documents, emails, invoices, deadlines, outcomes — connected, findable, governable. Nothing slips between systems because nothing lives outside the platform.

*Callback line:* The work itself is the record. Visibility and governance built in, not bolted on.

**Visual treatment:** Three columns, each with a thin divider between the operational claim and the callback line. No icons in v1 — typography carries the work. Light background as a deliberate breathing moment between dark Sections 2 and 4.

### Section 4 — The platform (no standalone heading)

**Transitional line below Section 3 cards (centered, lighter weight):**
Built on Microsoft, designed for legal — every capability connected to every matter and project.

**Five capability cards in zigzag layout (alternating image-right, image-left, image-right, image-left, image-right). Each card has a capability name, a one-sentence claim, and a Learn more link to a Platform page anchor:**

**01. Operations**
Matters, projects, tasks, and people — the operational backbone for the legal team and the work they own.

**02. Documents & Knowledge**
Every document, email, and contract — connected, searchable, and AI-aware. Built on SharePoint Embedded with Azure AI semantic search and Find Similar.

AI agents, automated workflows, and event-driven rules — the operational intelligence that runs in the background and shows up in Copilot.

**05. Spend & Performance**
Invoices, budgets, OCG compliance, and matter outcomes — the financial and operational truth about every matter and every firm.

**Microsoft foundation card (closes the section, full-width, distinct visual treatment with Microsoft logos):**

**Eyebrow:** Built on Microsoft
**Heading:** Microsoft, end to end.
**Body:** Spaarke runs natively on the Microsoft tools your team already uses, inside the security perimeter your IT team already approved. No new identity, no parallel governance.
**Logos (right side of card):** Power Platform, SharePoint, Outlook, Teams, M365 Copilot, Azure AI Foundry — official Microsoft brand assets.

**Section CTA (centered below the foundation card):** Explore the full platform →

**Production note:** The five capability cards each require a focused product screenshot — not the full workspace shot used in the hero. *Operations* needs the dashboard view; *Documents & Knowledge* needs document list with Find Similar or AI extraction visible; *Collaboration* needs shared workspace view with permissions; *Agents & Automation* needs the workflow builder or a Copilot prompt-and-response or an agent in action; *Spend & Performance* needs an invoice review or OCG compliance or matter scorecard view.

### Section 5 — Introducing Legal Operations Intelligence

**Section heading:** Introducing Legal Operations Intelligence.

**Intro paragraph:** Spaarke is the *Legal IQ* layer that makes Copilot, your AI agents, and your existing systems fluent in legal work. Decisions, workflows, performance, partnership — not just drafting.

**Layout:** The diagram is the visual centerpiece. Supporting copy is intentionally restrained — detailed architectural depth lives on the Platform page and the Why Spaarke page, not here.

**The diagram:**

A platform-container on the left, a directional arrow in the middle, and the M365 Copilot logo on the right. The visual claim is unambiguous: *Spaarke is the platform; Copilot is the destination; Spaarke flows into Copilot.*

- **Left side:** A bounded container labeled *Spaarke* (wordmark) with eyebrow text *LEGAL IQ PLATFORM*. Inside the container, a 2x4 grid of capability icons (Matters, Projects, Documents, Skills, Tools, Knowledge, Workflows, Performance — or equivalent). Container has a subtle gradient or accent treatment that signals it's the protagonist.
- **Middle:** A clean, elegant arrow flowing left-to-right from the Spaarke container into the Copilot logo. Color treatment can subtly transition from a Spaarke accent into the Copilot gradient, signaling handoff.
- **Right side:** The official Microsoft 365 Copilot brand asset (the colorful gradient mark) with a small *M365* tag and a soft radial accent glow behind it.

**Production note:** This diagram is the most strategic visual on the home page and will be reused in decks, RFP responses, analyst conversations, and IT security reviews. Investing in the polish — proper Copilot brand asset from Microsoft's brand resource center, refined arrow treatment, consistent icon style across the eight capability tiles, accessible text sizing — pays off across multiple use cases beyond the home page.

## Buyer context

The 2026 legal operations buyer is navigating pressure from three directions simultaneously: business clients expecting faster turnaround amid rising workload and complexity (77% per Axiom 2026); AI adoption that's outpaced AI maturity (only 1 in 5 departments have reached AI maturity, even as budgets rise); and outside counsel relationships demanding transparency that legacy tools can't provide (79% pressured to cut spend, 57% can't quantify savings, 87% of external budget walking out the door without real-time visibility). Spaarke is positioned to resolve all three pressures at once, by introducing a new category — Legal Operations Intelligence — that runs as the Legal IQ layer on top of Microsoft 365.

## Scope and evolution

This brief covers the **home page only**. Subsequent briefs should extend this template for:

- **Platform page** — the deeper product walkthrough, with each of the five capabilities getting its own deep section. Detailed architectural copy that was kept off the home page Section 5 lives here in full depth. The Microsoft foundation gets its own architectural section with security and integration detail.
- **Why Spaarke page** — the strategic argument with research, data, and the system-of-record case at depth. The CLOC and Axiom research, the productivity-gap argument, and the Legal Operations Intelligence category framing get the room they need.
- **Insights landing page** — the content library structure with filtering and featured pieces. Accessible from top nav.
- **Audience-specific pages — *For Legal Departments*, *For Outside Counsel*, *For Business Clients*** — these were considered for the home page (as the *Built for both sides* section in v1.2) but removed in v1.3. They become standalone Phase 2 pages once buyer feedback confirms which audience-specific framings convert. Note: with the v1.4 expansion to the three-stakeholder framing, a *For Business Clients* page may be a meaningful addition that wasn't on the v1.2 roadmap.

When updating this brief, increment the version number and date. Brand vocabulary, strategic position, and category claims should not be changed without explicit re-positioning agreement — they're the foundation that keeps the rest of the site coherent across iterations. Particular care: *See all sides of every matter*, *Legal Operations Intelligence*, and *Legal IQ* are new category-claiming language and need consistent reinforcement across every page, deck, and conversation for the first several months of usage to cement.
